"If I received that letter — say, from myself at twenty-seven — the first thing I would want to know is what kind of person wrote it. Was the older me proud of how I lived, or simply tired? Act I — The Signal from Kepler

short story · about 320 words · written for this lesson

The Signal from Kepler

At 23:47, Aaron saw it first. A clean pulse rising from a quiet patch of sky near Kepler-186 — six identical peaks marching across his oscilloscope, exactly eleven minutes apart. He turned to Reyes without speaking. Reyes finished his coffee, leaned over, watched the waveform for half a minute, and then said, "Record."

Aaron hit RECORD. The pulse vanished.

Not faded — vanished. The trace went flat the moment the indicator lit. He stopped the recording. Three minutes later, the pulse returned. The same rhythm. The same patch of sky.

"Try again," Reyes said.

Aaron tried again. Same result. RECORD light on — silence. RECORD light off — pulse.

They sat in the cold green glow of the monitors for a minute. The wind outside the hut hissed against the metal.

Reyes opened the field logbook on the desk between them — the one Mia had sent him from California when she retired, the one her grandfather had kept in 1987. He turned to a page he had read a dozen times. Aaron looked over his shoulder. The handwriting was careful, mechanical:

"She does not know yet. Tell her, gently, that she will. The watch she will bring me has no fault — only that it has been wound the wrong way for a very long time."

Aaron read the line twice. He looked back at his screen. The pulse was still there, six clean peaks, oblivious.

"It does not want to be recorded," Reyes said quietly. He was not asking.

Aaron did not answer. He was trying to remember whether the watchmaker had been talking about a watch, or about something else, all those years ago — and whether the difference mattered. He reached for his pen and opened a fresh page of the logbook.

The pulse was still there at 00:14 when he started writing.

Act II — Supervisor's lecture · Drake, Fermi, and three anomalies

about 90 seconds · listen twice · the script is hidden until after the questions

A short factual piece — Reyes's old supervisor, on archive video, summarising why scientists expect either no one or many. Take notes on three points: (1) what the Drake equation tries to estimate, (2) what the Fermi paradox actually asks, (3) the three patterns SETI cannot currently explain.

Script (open after the questions):
In 1961, the radio astronomer Frank Drake wrote down an equation that tried to estimate one number: how many communicating civilisations might exist in our galaxy right now. He multiplied the rate of star formation by the fraction of stars with planets, the fraction of those planets that are habitable, the fraction where life arises, the fraction where intelligence develops, the fraction that learns to send radio signals, and finally the lifetime of such a civilisation. Most of these factors are unknown by orders of magnitude. The Drake equation is not a prediction. It is, in his own words, "a way to organise our ignorance." Eleven years later, the physicist Enrico Fermi asked a sharper question, often paraphrased as: if intelligent civilisations are likely, where is everybody? The galaxy is more than ten billion years old; a single civilisation with patience could have colonised it many times over. Yet our sky, after sixty years of careful listening, is unremarkable. This is the Fermi paradox. Recently — and this is the reason we are awake tonight — three patterns have surfaced that we cannot explain. A repeating pulse near Kepler-186 that avoids being recorded. An unprinted letter that has arrived twice this year at two different observatories. And a small quadrant of sky, near Cygnus, that dampens any radio signal we send through it — as though someone is politely refusing to listen. None of these has been published. All three are in the file folder on your desk. Read it carefully before you say anything to anyone.

A · drop a heading here A A clean radio pulse appears in a quiet patch of sky toward Kepler-186, repeats six identical peaks at eleven-minute intervals, and disappears the instant any recording instrument is engaged. Two independent stations have observed it on three different nights this year. Standard interference checks have ruled out aircraft, satellites, lunar reflection, and equipment fault. B · drop a heading here B Outgoing transmissions aimed at a small region near Cygnus are systematically attenuated, sometimes by as much as eighteen decibels, regardless of frequency or power. Receivers in the same region report normal background noise, suggesting the dampening is directional rather than environmental. Three different observatories have confirmed the pattern across the past fifteen months. No celestial body or known phenomenon has been associated with the affected area.
C · drop a heading here C On two separate occasions this year, a continuous-form printer at a SETI station has produced several pages of clean English-language text without any operator having sent a print job. The printers were powered down at the time of the incident, both running on backup capacitors. The text purports to describe humanity from the perspective of an observer of two centuries. No source IP, no internal record, no power draw was logged.
D · drop a heading here D An undated marginal note in the field logbook of a private watchmaker, written in March 1987, references events that should have been unknown to the author at the time of writing. The note names a customer who would not be born for another nineteen years; describes their action; and concludes with an instruction to «tell her, gently.» The logbook was authenticated through paper analysis. Authorship was confirmed by the original surviving witness.

The paper was widely dismissed as algorithmically naïve. Within four years, two of the three rejected models were quietly retracted from the literature on grounds the same paper had raised. Describe a moment when something familiar suddenly meant something new.

For me — cloze answer key (A)open if needed
  1. A — years (точный временной отрезок «two hundred years», не «seasons/decades/months»)
  2. B — output (radio output = совокупность RF-излучения; trick: «broadcast» — конкретный передача, «noise» — нет ассоциации с «loud and badly insulated», «trail» — не для радио)
  3. C — consider («consider + Object + Complement» = считать кого-то кем-то; trick: «regard» нужно «as», «think» нужно «to be», «imagine» — несмыкаемое)
  4. D — broadcasting (radio era ~1920, «less than a century» — это про передачу сигнала в космос)
  5. A — ready (collocation «ready for it»; trick: «trained for» нужно конкретное умение, «waiting for» — sense of expectation, «longing for» — желание/тоска)
  6. B — accidental (антоним «considered» = намеренный/обдуманный; trick: «empty» — фактическое отсутствие, не лексическая пара; «random» близко, но слабее как контраст; «natural» — другое направление)

Q1 — First action. The first thing I do is stay calm and not touch a single button. I run the standard verification protocol — re-check the signal on at least two independent receivers, look for terrestrial sources, satellite reflections, instrument errors. ✅ True / False · 8 statements
1Reyes's team detected exactly one anomalous pulse during the night of the incident.
False. Three anomalies were recorded, all provisionally classified as Class-S.
2Mia's 2019 paper on unresolved SETI anomalies was well received at the time.
False. It was dismissed as naïve; vindication came later.
3The waveform vanished the moment any recorder was engaged.
True. The behaviour was systematically unpredictable — evasion of measurement, not random.
4Aaron argued for an immediate public release; Reyes argued for a delay.
True. Two positions inside the same team; Reyes prevailed with a twelve-week delay.
5The Drake equation predicts a small number of communicating civilisations in our galaxy.
False. Optimistic estimates put the galaxy as «crowded» — that mismatch with observed silence is the Fermi paradox.
6Mia's grandfather's logbook physically lay on the operations desk during the night.
True. This is a deliberate L1 callback — the same logbook, now witnessing a very different anomaly.
7Reyes signed off her transmission with «I take full responsibility for tonight's decision to publish nothing».
True. Exact wording; note the passive-authority construction — «to publish nothing» rather than «to withhold».
8The lecture presents «we are alone» as the only scientifically credible interpretation of the silence.
False. The lecture keeps two hypotheses live: «we are early» and «self-destruction filter». No single position is endorsed.
