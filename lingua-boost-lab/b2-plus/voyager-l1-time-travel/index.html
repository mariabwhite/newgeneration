Lesson 1 · Time

A letter from a watch, and a crack in light itself

An original short story about a granddaughter and a logbook that knows too much; a 90-second listening on what real physics actually says about time bending; and one olympiad-style question that pulls both together. We finish with a short piece of writing — done here, on the lesson, not at home.

Voyager · a 4-episode quest

One man's life, told through the four instruments that finally let him speak — a watch, a signal, a blink, a letter.

  1. L1 · Watchmaker (you're here). Winter 1995. An old clockmaker, his granddaughter Mia, and a stranger of thirty who came in to fix a watch. That night the clockmaker writes one line about the visitor in the family logbook: "Mind is still wound." The visitor's name is Theo Marshall.
  2. L2 · Are we alone? Twenty years on. Mia — grown up — sits at a SETI console in the Atacama. Her senior on the shift, Dr Naomi Reyes, reads the final broadcast. What Proxima returns is not a message but a rhythm: three short, two long, every nineteen seconds — the same rhythm the clockmaker once drew.
  3. L3 · Whose mind? Naomi has crossed the corridor from SETI to neurology. Her newest patient has been locked-in for three years — and is blinking on the same nineteen-second rhythm. Eleven weeks of blinks spell the watchmaker's line, letter by letter.
  4. L4 · The night the letter arrived. Day 42 after Naomi goes public with the case. An envelope is placed by the patient's bed. At 3:17 a.m. he blinks one word — FATHER. Naomi had known who he was all along.

Warm-up — speaking

Imagine a letter arrives, addressed to you, from yourself ten years older. ♪ tap to hear

Talk for about two minutes. Don't plan — just start. Then read the sample model below and steal one or two phrases for next time.

Model · steal a phrase

"If I received that letter — say, from myself at twenty-seven — the first thing I would want to know is what kind of person wrote it. Was the older me proud of how I lived, or simply tired? I think I would trust advice less than I would trust a warning, because advice can be wrong, but a warning is usually written by someone who has already lost. The most powerful thing about the letter would not be its content, but the fact that, somewhere, a version of me had the patience to sit down and address me as a stranger."

Why these phrases: they show three olympiad moves — (1) reframe the question, (2) prefer a sharper word (advice vs warning), (3) lift the focus from what to that. Borrow one for Section 7.

03

Reading — short story

about 320 words · written for this lesson

The Watchmaker's Apology

Mia's grandfather had been a watchmaker for fifty-three years before the accident. He could disassemble a pocket watch in the dark and rebuild it by morning. After the stroke, he could no longer hold a screwdriver, and he stopped speaking almost entirely. Mia visited him every Sunday and read aloud from his old logbooks — careful, dated entries about which gears he had cleaned and which springs he had replaced.

One Sunday in November, while reading the entry for 14 March 1987, Mia noticed something she had read past for months. The customer for that day was a young woman named — Mia Andreevna Markova. Mia's own name. Her own patronymic. Her own surname.

Below the customer's name, in her grandfather's careful handwriting, was a short note:

"She does not know yet. Tell her, gently, that she will. The watch she will bring me has no fault — only that it has been wound the wrong way for a very long time."

Mia set the logbook down. She was eighteen years old. The date 14 March 1987 was nineteen years before her own birth.

Her grandfather, who had not spoken for four months, opened his eyes and looked at her.

"Mia," he said, very slowly. "Do not be afraid of the date. You will go. You will not change anything that matters. You will only — adjust."

She asked him what he meant. He smiled the way a watchmaker smiles when the spring finally clicks into place — and closed his eyes again.

By the next Sunday, the logbook entry was still there. The handwriting was still his. But the name on the line for 14 March had changed. It now read, in slightly fresher ink: delivered, returned safely, never asked again.

Quick check

1
Mia's grandfather had stopped speaking before the Sunday she found her own name in the logbook.
2
The grandfather's note suggests Mia will travel back to him because she chooses to.
3
According to the grandfather, what is Mia allowed to do when she goes?
4
Why does the logbook entry change between Sundays?
For me — answer keys (Quick check) open if needed
  1. True — grandfather had stopped speaking before that Sunday («for four months»).
  2. False — the grandfather frames it as inevitability («You will go»), not a free choice.
  3. b — «adjust but not change anything important».
  4. c — Mia has gone and come back; the trip is now part of the recorded past.

04

Listening — what physics actually says about time

about 90 seconds · listen twice · the script is hidden until after the questions

A short factual piece. Take notes on three points: (1) what the 2011 CERN experiment claimed, (2) the GPS correction figure, (3) Hawking's position on direction of time travel.

Script (open after the questions):
In 2011, a team at CERN — the European centre for particle physics in Geneva — fired a beam of tiny particles called neutrinos toward a detector 730 kilometres away in Italy. The result, when it came in, was so strange that the scientists rechecked it 15,000 times. The neutrinos seemed to arrive sixty nanoseconds — that is, sixty billionths of a second — earlier than light would have. If the result was real, one of physics's most cherished rules — that nothing travels faster than light — had to be rethought. The result was later traced to a faulty cable in the experiment. But the question it asked is still alive. Einstein's relativity tells us that clocks closer to a large mass — like a planet — tick more slowly than clocks far above. This is not just a theory; it is the reason your phone needs to add about thirty-eight microseconds to its GPS signal every day. Without that correction, your map would drift by ten kilometres in twenty-four hours. So time, in physics, is not the steady backdrop most of us imagine. It bends near gravity. It runs at different rates for travellers at different speeds. The physicist Stephen Hawking once said that travel into the future — even by millions of years — would, in principle, be allowed by these laws. Travel into the past, he believed, was off limits: it would break the rule that a cause must come before its effect.

Lexical keys (1–6):

  1. B — feels (collocation: «time feels»)
  2. A — sure («quietly sure that…»)
  3. B — Since (causal: «since Einstein, the same speed cannot…»)
  4. A — so («so X that Y» construction)
  5. A — into («build it into their math»)
  6. A — said (neutral reporting verb)

Grammatical keys (7–10):

  1. while (или whereas, but) — contrast
  2. by (или under) — preposition with «which» relative clause
  3. the — definite article with «way + relative clause»
  4. not — negation in parallel structure

Что тренирует: collocation, дискурсивные коннекторы (since vs before), constructions so…that, артикли с уникальной референцией, предлоги с relative clauses. Все 4 «открытых» пропуска — типичная грамматика школьного/муниципального этапа.

B · Sentence transformation (4 items)

Rewrite the second sentence so it means the same as the first, using the word in CAPITALS. Write between 2 and 4 words. The capitalised word must stay unchanged.

1. Hawking said that travel into the past would never be allowed.
According to Hawking, travel into the past WOULD ____________ be allowed.
Model: would never
2. Nobody had ever measured a particle faster than light before this experiment.
This experiment was the FIRST ____________ a particle had been measured faster than light.
Model: first time
3. The grandfather did not explain his reasons in any detail.
The grandfather AVOIDED ____________ his reasons in any detail.
Model: avoided explaining (or just explaining)
4. If the cable had not been faulty, the result would have been different.
Had the cable NOT ____________, the result would have been different.
Model: not been faulty
For me — what this trains open if needed
  • Cloze trains collocation + register: fascinated by, traced to, closed the distance, definite article with abstract effects. Real ВсОШ school-stage format.
  • Transformation trains: modal+adverb order (would never), the first time + present perfect, avoid + gerund, inversion in mixed conditionals.
  • If Vanya угадывает не вспоминая правило — спроси «почему именно это, а не C?» — он сам перейдёт от чутья к объяснению. Это то что меряют олимпиадные жюри.
