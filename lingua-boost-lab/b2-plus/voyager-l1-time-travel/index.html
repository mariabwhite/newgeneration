Time travel: Plumber × Neutrinos × Hawking

01

Warm-up — speaking

two minutes · no preparation · just say what comes

Imagine a letter arrives, addressed to you, from yourself ten years older.

Talk for about two minutes. Don't plan — just start:

  • What does the letter say in the first line?
  • Does future-you give advice, or only warn you about something?
  • Do you trust the letter? Why or why not?
Why we start here: the whole lesson is about messages reaching us across time. The Plumber story and the IELTS article both ask the same quiet question — what would it actually mean to receive information from a different point in time?

Reading A — fiction · «The time travel plumber»

a short story by Nicola Prentis · British Council, B2/C1 · read it there, come back here
Story · ~1400 words Author · Nicola Prentis Level · B2/C1

Priya's kitchen is flooded, her son Charlie is ill, and a plumber arrives from an insurance company called TimeTech — claiming to come from the future. The slogan on his van promises to undo every problem you've ever had. But there is a footnote, in tiny print, that Priya doesn't read until later. Once she believes time travel is real, she decides to act on it — and the story turns.

Open the story on British Council
Reading goal: notice (a) what TimeTech promises, (b) what the small print actually excludes, and (c) the choice Priya makes when she believes the service is real. Don't worry about every word — return here once you understand the timeline.

Quick check — did you follow the story?

three quick T / F questions
1
TimeTech sends its plumber back from the future to fix problems that have already happened in the customer's life.
2
According to the small print on the van, TimeTech can rewind situations caused by human actions, not just by broken objects.
3
By the end of the story, Priya feels her old worries have been replaced by a new one she did not expect.

Reading B — science · IELTS Academic «Time Travel»

British Council IELTS Reading practice · Section 3 · the same idea, but in physics
Article · ~700 words Source · British Council IELTS Level · B2/C1

An IELTS-style article about a 2011 experiment at CERN in which neutrinos appeared to travel from Geneva to a detector 730 km away in Italy slightly faster than light. If true, the result would shake one of physics' most cherished rules. The article then walks through the famous grandfather paradox, three rival theories (self-consistency, many-worlds, non-existence) and ends with Stephen Hawking's careful, half-optimistic view on whether humans might ever travel through time.

Open the article on British Council
Reading goal: as you read, note (a) what the OPERA experiment actually measured, (b) what each of the four time-travel theories says briefly, and (c) Hawking's specific position — which direction in time he thinks could in principle work.

08

Synthesis — fiction vs science

ВсОШ-style critical comparison · 6–8 sentences

In the Plumber story, TimeTech repairs physical objects but refuses to undo human actions. In the IELTS article, Igor Novikov argues that a traveller can affect history but not change it.

Answer in 6–8 sentences:

  1. What is the difference between "objects vs actions" (Plumber rule) and "affect vs change" (Novikov)?
  2. Which limit is stricter? Give one example each limit could allow but the other could not.
  3. Do you find one of the two more believable? Why?

10

ЕГЭ-style — match the headings

four short paragraphs · five headings · one is extra
1 · The mistake your phone never makes 2 · The traveller who returns younger 3 · Why physics treats time as a one-way street 4 · Time as symmetry at the smallest scale 5 · The clock that runs slower the deeper you go
A · drop a heading here A Satellites orbiting the Earth move so fast that the clocks they carry tick slightly slower than clocks on the ground. If engineers did not add about 38 microseconds back into the GPS signal every day, your phone's location would drift by ten kilometres in twenty-four hours. Einstein's general relativity is, quite literally, in your pocket.
B · drop a heading here B Imagine two twins. One flies a spaceship close to the speed of light; the other stays at home. When the traveller comes back, she is younger than her brother. The asymmetry is not magic — it comes from the fact that the traveller's frame of reference accelerated, while the stay-at-home brother's did not.
C · drop a heading here C The second law of thermodynamics says that disorder always grows in a closed system. We remember the past, not the future, because the past is the direction of lower disorder. Some physicists therefore say there is no fundamental "flow" of time — only a statistical arrow pointing from order to mess.
D · drop a heading here D At the smallest scale, the equations of quantum mechanics treat time as symmetric. A film of a single electron played backwards looks just as physically valid as one played forwards. The everyday sense of "time passing" seems to emerge only when very many particles interact at once.
