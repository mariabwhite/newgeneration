13-19Reading · T/F/NS · 7 statements

Birthdays look very different around the world, but every country agrees on one idea — the day a person was born is worth celebrating. In Russia, many families still bake a sweet bread called karavay for important birthdays, and most parties include a big cake with candles. The birthday person makes a silent wish, blows out the candles, and the wish, the tradition says, will come true only if it stays secret.

In Mexico, children love a colourful tradition called the piñata. A large paper figure full of sweets is hung from a tree or a ceiling. Each guest takes a stick, closes their eyes, and tries to break the piñata so that all the sweets fall on the floor. The whole game is loud, funny and a little dangerous, but the children love it.

In Japan, the family\'s style of celebration depends a lot on the age of the birthday person. For older relatives, the family often cooks special red rice called sekihan. For younger children, parents prefer Western-style cakes and pop music. In recent years, online birthday meetings with relatives in other cities have also become popular among Japanese teenagers.

What is universal about birthdays is the human need to feel important. A handmade gift, a song sung by friends, a phone call from a grandparent — these small details matter much more than the price of the present. Across cultures, the message is the same: you matter to us, and today we celebrate the fact that you exist.

Practice · Extra speaking cards

Six discussion cards on our topics. Use the answer frame: Direct answer → Reason → Example → Personal detail. 40-60 seconds each. Tap 🔊 to hear the question.

P-S6 cards · 40-60 sec each · answer frame
1. Should teenagers help with the housework? Why? Yes, teenagers should definitely help with the housework, because our parents work hard all day and sharing the chores is a fair way to thank them. For example, in my family I take out the rubbish and help with cooking on Saturdays. Personally, I don't always enjoy doing the washing-up, but I never refuse — it's part of the deal. 2. Are big birthday parties a waste of money? I partly agree. A huge restaurant party with expensive decorations can really be a waste, because the next day there is nothing left except photos. For example, my best birthday was a small picnic in the park with five friends — handmade gifts and one cake. Personally, I prefer small celebrations because they feel more meaningful. 3. What can young people do to protect the environment? Young people can do a lot, even at home. We should sort the rubbish, save energy by turning off the lights, and use reusable bottles instead of plastic. For example, our class organised a "no-plastic Friday" last term. Personally, I believe small daily actions matter more than one big eco-event a year. 4. Boys and girls — should they share the same chores? Yes, I strongly believe boys and girls should share the same chores. Vacuuming the carpet is not "girls' work", and fixing a lamp is not "boys' work". For example, in my family my brother does the laundry and my mum fixes things. Personally, I think the home is cleaner when everyone helps with everything. 5. Is climate change really a serious problem for our generation? Yes, climate change is one of the most serious problems for our generation, because we will live with the consequences for the longest time. For example, summers are already hotter than ten years ago, and some species are becoming endangered. Personally, I think every teenager should learn at least the basics of ecology at school. 6. Handmade gifts vs shop-bought — which is better? I definitely prefer handmade gifts, because they show that you spent time thinking about the person. For example, last year my friends made me a photo album from our school trip — I still look at it. Personally, I think the price of a present matters much less than the feeling behind it.

SP1Read aloud · ~9 sentences

In many Russian schools, the eco club has become one of the most popular activities after lessons. Students collect plastic bottles, organise clean-up walks around the neighbourhood, and learn how recycling really works. They also talk about biodiversity and climate change in classes that mix science, music and even sport. Some clubs invite local champions to support outdoor events in the city park near the school. A few schools have started to celebrate "zero-rubbish" birthdays, where the children exchange handmade gifts instead of plastic toys. Through small actions, students get used to thinking carefully about the environment. Teachers say that this is one of the best ways to grow real responsibility. Small steps, repeated every week, can change the whole school culture.

Speaking · Task 2 · Phone survey · 6 questions · 40 s each

You are taking part in a phone survey about hobbies and free time. Answer six questions. After the signal, you have 40 seconds for each answer.

SP26 questions · 40 sec each

"Hello! We are doing a survey about teenagers, home life and the environment. Please answer six questions. After the signal you will have 40 seconds for each answer. The interview will not take much of your time."

1. What household chores do you usually do at home? At home I usually take out the rubbish, make my bed every morning, and on Saturdays I help my mum with the cooking. I don't love doing the washing-up, but I never refuse — it's part of the deal. 2. How often do you and your family recycle paper or plastic? We recycle almost every day. We have three small bins in the kitchen — for paper, plastic and glass. Once a week, my dad takes everything to the recycling point near our house. 3. How do you and your family usually celebrate birthdays? We throw a small party at home. My mum bakes a big cake, my friends come over, we play board games and we always try to bring handmade gifts. The candles and the wish are the most important part for me. 4. Why is it important for teenagers to help their parents at home? It's important because our parents work hard all day, and sharing the chores is a fair way to thank them. Also, learning to cook and clean is a useful skill for our own future life. 5. Some people say that big birthday parties are a waste of money. Do you agree? I partly agree. A huge restaurant party with expensive decorations can really be a waste. But a small home party with friends and handmade gifts costs almost nothing — and people remember it for years. 6. What advice would you give to a friend who wants to live in a more eco-friendly way? I would advise them to start small. Use a reusable bottle, recycle paper and plastic, walk or cycle to school. You don't have to change everything at once — one new habit a month is already a lot.

SP3Monologue · 12+ phrases · TV vs Internet
Topic · Protecting the environment

"Imagine that you are doing a project «Protecting the environment». You are gathering information for your project and would like to share it with your friends. Speak for not more than 2 minutes (10-12 sentences)."

Remember to say:

  1. what environmental problems are the most serious in your area;
  2. what people can do at home or at school to help the environment;
  3. whether you think young people can really make a difference, and why;
  4. what your attitude to recycling and other green habits is.
Your turn — record your 2-minute monologue:
I'm going to give a talk about protecting the environment. In my area, the most serious problems are air pollution and rubbish on the streets. There are too many cars, and not all people throw their waste in the bins. In summer, the air sometimes feels heavy, and you can see smog above the city. But people can do a lot, both at home and at school. At home, families should save water, recycle paper and plastic, and turn off the lights when they leave a room. At school, we can organise eco projects, plant trees in the school yard, and bring reusable bottles instead of buying plastic ones. I really believe that young people can make a real difference. We are the generation that will live with the consequences for the longest time, so we have the strongest reason to act. Of course, one person alone is not enough — but when thousands of students do small things every day, the result is huge. As for my own attitude, I think recycling and other green habits should not feel like a punishment — they must become a normal part of daily life. I am proud that my family separates the rubbish, and I love our school's no-plastic week. In conclusion, protecting the environment is not someone else's job — it is ours. That's all I wanted to say. Thank you for listening!
