Part 1 · The four present tenses

? Present Simple — facts that stay true

Formula I / you / we / they visit
he / she / it visits
When to use it A fact about the world. A daily routine. Something that's always-true. Time words: every day, usually, often, sometimes, never, on Sundays.
Temple examples · ?
"The Golden Temple opens its doors to people of every faith."
"Pilgrims visit the Western Wall every single day."

? Present Continuous — happening RIGHT NOW

Formula am / is / are + verb-ing
When to use it An action in progress at the moment of speaking — or around this time in your life. Time words: now, right now, at the moment, today, this week.
Temple examples · ?
"Right now the monks at Wat Pho are polishing the gold on the Buddha's feet."
"A pilgrim is pressing a tiny paper prayer into a crack of the Western Wall."

? Present Perfect — finished action, the result is HERE NOW

Formula have / has + V3 (past participle)
e.g. have visited, has changed
When to use it An action that finished in the past but the result still matters now. Also: life experience ("ever / never"), count up-to-now ("three times"), recent action with "just". Time words: already, just, yet, ever, never, this week, in my life.
Temple examples · ?
"Hagia Sophia has changed religions three times."
"Anya has just lit her first candle at the ghats."
"I have already visited four sacred sites this trip."

? Present Perfect Continuous — duration, still going on

Formula have / has + been + verb-ing
When to use it An action that started in the past, is still going on now (or only just stopped). The focus is HOW LONG. Always with for + a length or since + a starting point.
Temple examples · ?
"Pilgrims have been bathing in the Ganges for 3000 years."
"Builders have been working on the Sagrada Familia since 1882."
? The classic trap · Perfect vs Perfect Continuous "Hagia Sophia has stood for 1500 years." > Present Perfect — the FACT of standing, the result.
"Builders have been working on Sagrada Familia for 140 years." > Present Perfect Continuous — the PROCESS, the ongoing action, it's still happening.
Tip: if the verb describes a state (be, know, have) — use Present Perfect. If it's an action you can picture in progress — use Present Perfect Continuous.

Part 2 · Two ways to talk about the future

? WILL — decision made RIGHT NOW · promise · gut feeling

Formula will + verb (base form)
negative: won't
short form: 'll
When to use it • A decision made in the moment of speaking ("Don't worry, I'll get it.")
• A promise ("I'll send you a postcard.")
• A prediction from the heart, without proof ("I think she'll love it.")
• Spontaneous offer ("I'll hold your hand.")
Temple examples · ?
"Don't worry, mum — I 'll send you a postcard from every city. Promise."
"Tomorrow Anya will drop a coin into each of 108 bowls at Wat Pho."

? BE GOING TO — plan made BEFORE · prediction with EVIDENCE

Formula am / is / are + going to + verb
e.g. is going to rain
When to use it • A plan or intention you decided BEFORE the conversation ("I'm going to visit Cambodia next summer — saved up two years.")
• A prediction based on visible evidence ("Look at those clouds — it's going to rain.")
Temple examples · ?
"Tonight volunteers are going to feed 100,000 pilgrims at the Golden Temple."
"Look at those grey clouds above Varanasi — it 's going to rain tonight."
? The split-second rule Ask yourself: "Did I decide this before we started talking?"
> YES (already a plan, ticket bought, scaffolding up, plan in my head) > use be going to.
> NO (I'm deciding right now, in this moment, hearing what you just said) > use will.
Bonus: predictions. Visible proof in front of you (clouds > rain) > going to. Just a feeling, opinion, hope > will.

The scene: You've taken off your shoes, covered your head, and walked across the cold marble path to the Golden Temple's langar — the free kitchen that has been feeding everyone, of every faith, every single day, for nearly five hundred years. An elderly volunteer in an orange turban hands you a steel plate. He smiles. "I have been serving here every Sunday for thirty-two years," he says. You sit down. Three things you could say to him:
A
"Thirty-two years? What keeps you coming back?"
Loyalty · âåðíîñòü You honour the long quiet thread of his service. He smiles a slow smile. "When I lost my wife, this kitchen kept me standing. Now I stand for it." Loyalty isn't loud — sometimes it just means showing up, every Sunday, for thirty-two years.
B
"Honestly — why do you feed people who don't even share your faith?"
Honest · ÷åñòíûé You ask the real question, gently. He looks at you for a long moment. "Because hunger doesn't ask about faith. So why should we?" That's the honest answer — and the whole point of langar.
C
"…can I help wash the plates after?"
Spirit · äóõ You don't ask anything. You just offer your hands. He nods, eyes warm. Two hours later you are still there, up to your elbows in soapy water, laughing with three strangers. You felt the spirit of the place — and joined it.

Will or Going to? · planning the next leg

?? Future move · 6 mini-questions
WILL = a decision right now, a promise, or a guess from the heart. BE GOING TO = a plan you already made before, or a prediction with visible evidence. Tap one — green is right.

? The split second of difference

WILL · decided right now — "I'm thirsty."
— "Don't move, I 'll get you some chai."
Decision made in the moment.
BE GOING TO · already a plan "Anya bought a ticket last week. She 's going to fly to Bangkok on Friday."
Plan exists before the conversation.
1. Look at those grey clouds above Varanasi. It ___ rain tonight.
> visible evidence (grey clouds) > BE GOING TO
2. — I have no Egyptian pounds for the taxi! — Don't worry, I ___ pay for both of us.
> decision made at this exact moment > WILL
3. Anya has bought her ticket. She ___ leave Cairo for Varanasi on Friday.
> plan made before now (ticket already bought) > BE GOING TO
4. Don't worry, mum. I ___ send you a postcard from every single city. Promise.
> a promise > WILL
5. The kitchen at the Golden Temple is already buzzing — volunteers ___ feed 100,000 pilgrims tonight.
> planned ritual you can see preparing > BE GOING TO
6. — Which temple should we visit first in Bangkok? — Hmm… I think we ___ start with Wat Pho.
> decision in the moment, with "I think" > WILL
7. The scaffolding is already up — workers ___ restore the dome of Hagia Sophia next month.
> visible evidence (scaffolding) + already planned > BE GOING TO
8. — I'm scared to walk barefoot on the marble. — Don't worry, I ___ hold your hand.
> a kind offer made in the moment > WILL
9. Anya has saved up for two years. Next summer she ___ travel to Cambodia to see Angkor Wat.
> a long-prepared plan (two years of saving) > BE GOING TO
10. I'm not sure, but I think the Sagrada Familia ___ be finished by 2030.
> a guess / prediction from the heart, not based on evidence > WILL

Tomorrow Anya is going to leave Bangkok. She has bought her ticket to Amritsar already. But this morning, she thinks, she will not take a single photo. Some things are better kept inside the eyes. The fox finishes the water, looks up once more, and disappears into the trees.
