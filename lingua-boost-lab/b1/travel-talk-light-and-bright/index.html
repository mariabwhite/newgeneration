Travel vocab · Types of bags

Tap any card to flip · listen and remember

trolley-bag
a small case on wheels that you pull behind you
"I only travel with a trolley-bag."
suitcase
a big rectangular case for clothes when travelling
"My suitcase is too heavy."
tote bag
a large open bag with two handles, often made of cloth
"I keep my book in a tote bag."
handbag
a small bag a woman uses to carry personal things
"Where did I put my handbag?"
backpack
a bag you carry on your back, with two shoulder straps
"A backpack is best for long trips."
trunk
a very large strong box used for travel (or storage)
"Her old trunk was from the 1950s."

Small Talk Magic · 5 mini-dialogues

Tap any line to hear · then read together with your partner

1. At the Airport Waiting Lounge
YouExcuse me, is this seat taken?
StrangerOh no, go ahead.
YouThanks! Is your flight on time?
StrangerSo far, yes. Fingers crossed!
YouSame here. I'm heading to Berlin. You?
StrangerBarcelona — first time there.
YouThat sounds amazing!
2. On a Train
YouDo you know how long this ride to Oxford usually takes?
StrangerAbout an hour and a half, I think.
YouPerfect, just enough time for a podcast.
StrangerOr a nap if you're lucky!
YouTrue! Early trains always make me sleepy.
3. At a Bus Station
YouHave you taken this route before?
StrangerA couple of times — it's a beautiful ride.
YouThat's good to hear. I'm new to this city.
StrangerYou'll love it! Friendly people and great food.
YouSounds promising. Thanks for the tip!
4. Waiting in Line at a Hotel Reception
YouQuite a queue, huh?
StrangerYeah, must be check-in rush hour.
YouI just got in from the airport. You too?
StrangerNo, I've been here a few days. It's a great city.
YouNice. Any recommendations for dinner nearby?
5. On a Ferry
YouBeautiful view, isn't it?
StrangerAbsolutely. Nothing beats sea breeze.
YouDo you travel by ferry often?
StrangerOnly when I visit my parents. They live on the island.
YouThat's lovely. Must be peaceful.
Talk: Which of these five dialogues happens to you most often in real life?

Role-Play Lab

Two situations · take turns · stay in character

Situation: You're at the airport. Your flight has been delayed by 3 hours. You start a conversation with the passenger sitting next to you.

Student A — You

  • Ask if they're also waiting for the same flight
  • Complain (politely) about the delay
  • Ask where they're going and why
  • Talk about how you usually pass the time when travelling

Student B — Stranger

  • Answer their questions
  • Mention where you're flying to and why (holiday, work, etc.)
  • Talk about a previous experience with travel delays
  • Recommend something to do while waiting
Situation: You're lost in a foreign city and need directions to the train station. You stop a local person on the street.

Student A — Tourist

  • Greet and ask politely for help
  • Explain you're looking for the train station
  • Ask how long it will take to walk
  • Ask about transport options if it's far

Student B — Local Person

  • Give directions clearly (e.g. "Turn left, go straight ahead…")
  • Recommend the best way to get there
  • Tell them how long it will take
  • Suggest a nice café or shop nearby

Phrasal Verbs of Travel

Tap a card to flip · then use 3 phrasal verbs in your own sentence

Mini grammar focus: phrasal verb = verb + particle (check + in, take + off). The meaning changes — you can't guess it word-by-word. Learn them as one block.
check in
to register on arrival (airport, hotel)
"I'll check in online before the flight."
check out
to leave a hotel (pay and go)
"We need to check out by 11 a.m."
take off
(of a plane) to leave the ground
"Our plane takes off at six."
set off
to begin a journey
"We set off at dawn to beat the traffic."
get away
to escape for a short break
"We just need to get away for the weekend."
look around
to explore a place
"Let's look around before lunch."
drop by
to visit briefly without planning
"We dropped by the old church on the way."
head back
to return / go home
"It's late — let's head back to the hotel."

▸ Now: tell your partner about your last trip using 3 phrasal verbs from above.
