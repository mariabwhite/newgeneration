What the updated TOEFL test measures

The updated TOEFL iBT assesses the four language skills — reading, listening, writing, speaking — and gives universities a reliable picture of your English in academic and campus-life contexts. Tasks are drawn from textbooks, newspapers, websites, lectures, announcements, emails and online discussions — short but authentic.

The test takes under two hours in total. It can be taken at home or at a test centre. Official scores arrive within 72 hours.

Two things to remember about the format

  • Reading and Listening are multistage adaptive. Your performance on the first part decides the difficulty of the second part — the test fits itself to your level.
  • Order is fixed: Reading → Listening → Writing → Speaking. You cannot go back to a previous section.
Why this matters: on test day you can't return to a section once it is closed. Plan your energy — Reading and Listening burn focus; Writing eats your hands; Speaking comes last when you are tired. In practice we will mirror that order.

Reading I · Complete the Words

Paragraph-length academic text (~70–100 words). After the intact first sentence, the second half of every second word is deleted. Restore the missing letters.

ETS sample task · 10 gaps

Fill in the missing letters in the paragraph below. The first sentence is given intact.

We know from drawings that have been preserved in caves for over 10,000 years that early humans performed dances as a group activity. We mi think th prehistoric peo concentrated on on ba survival. How, it i clear fr the rec that dan was important to them. They recorded more drawings of dances than any other group activity. Dances served various purposes, including ritualistic communication with the divine, storytelling, and social cohesion.
Strategy: read the whole paragraph first to catch the topic, then fill gaps in context. The missing letters always finish a real English word — say each candidate aloud.

Reading III · Read an Academic Passage

~200-word expository text from history, art, science, business, etc. Five multiple-choice questions on factual detail, vocabulary, inference, and rhetorical structure.

ETS sample task · Mirror Test

The Mirror Test

Very young children cannot recognize themselves in a mirror; they usually achieve this milestone around 18 months of age. The ability to recognize oneself in the mirror is considered to be a key component of self-awareness and consciousness for humans. But what about animals?

For many years, scientists have known that members of the great ape family could recognize themselves in mirrors. They measured this by the "mirror test," which involved putting a colored mark on an ape's body, and then showing the ape its reflection in a mirror. If the ape tried to remove the mark on its own body, the scientists knew that the ape was recognizing its reflection.

Apes are close relatives of humans, but in recent years, scientists have discovered that other animals also pass the "mirror test." Elephants and dolphins have shown signs of self-recognition. These, like apes, are highly intelligent animals. But in a more recent experiment, a type of fish called the cleaner fish tried to scrape a mark off its body when it saw itself in the mirror. This suggests that even less intelligent animals may possess more self-awareness than previously suspected.

ETS Reading · academic passage format
1. Why did scientists put colored marks on animals' bodies?
→ Direct restatement of the text: "If the ape tried to remove the mark on its own body, the scientists knew that the ape was recognizing its reflection."
2. Which idea is the passage building toward at the end?
→ Final sentence is the rhetorical punchline: "even less intelligent animals may possess more self-awareness than previously suspected." Main-idea questions follow the last paragraph's "this suggests…" move.
Strategy: Academic Passage rewards structural reading. Sentence 1 of each paragraph is usually the claim; the rest is support. End-of-passage sentences carry the main idea.

Listening II · Listen to a Conversation

A short conversation between two speakers. Listen for main idea, key details, inferred meaning and speaker purpose. Two questions follow.

ETS sample task · supermarket / play

Tap ▶ to listen. Try the question below first — only then open the script to verify.

▶ Conversation
📜 Open conversation script

Woman: Need anything from the supermarket?

Man: Huh? Aren't we getting ready to go see that play in a few minutes?

Woman: That's tomorrow.

Man: Oh. Wow, I'd forget my head if it wasn't screwed on… Guess I don't need to change my clothes after all.

Woman: So, you weren't planning to prepare dinner?

Man: No, but I can. What do you want?

Woman: Just something light and healthy. So, can you go shopping instead?

Man: Yeah, sure. How about salmon and salad? Want anything else?

Woman: No, that's good. Thanks!

1. What does the woman imply she was about to do?
→ "Need anything from the supermarket?" is offered as she's about to leave for the supermarket. The man then volunteers, and she accepts ("So, can you go shopping instead?").
Strategy: Conversation questions love implied answers. Don't pick the literal first option — pick the one that explains why the speaker said what they said.

Listening III · Listen to an Announcement

Short academic-related announcement (~40–85 words) — classroom, event, schedule, rule. Questions test main idea, detail and speaker purpose.

Listening IV · Listen to an Academic Talk

~100–250-word academic talk on art, history, science, business etc. Four questions on main and supporting ideas, organisation, inference, vocabulary.

ETS sample task · podcast on psychology · hard / soft fascination
▶ Academic talk
Transcript

Did you see that new thriller movie that came out last week? I did and loved it. The action, the plot twists… I was totally captivated. Time just flew by. Not a single thought occurred to me that was unrelated to the movie. What I experienced is what psychologists call hard fascination. Hard fascination means intense focus and concentration. Whether it's TV programs, video games… hard fascination is all too easy to come by in this modern world.

There's another type of fascination — soft fascination. There's still effortless attention, meaning that no special effort is required for you to stay focused, but there's still room for other thoughts. When I take a walk in the park and look at the flowers and trees, for example, I might be thinking in the back of my mind about my dinner plans.

Now, one thing to know is hard fascination causes mental fatigue. The mind is so intensely focused that it gets tired fast. What follows mental fatigue? You might find yourself easily distracted, irritable, and stressed. Soft fascination, in contrast, engages a different part of the brain — the DMN, or Default Mode Network, which soothes the mind and helps combat mental fatigue. So next time you feel like your mind is on overload, turn off the TV, put down your phone. Take a walk, or simply sit and stare at the clouds.

1. Why does the speaker mention a movie?
→ The movie is a hook, not the topic. The speaker uses it to introduce the term hard fascination. "Speaker purpose" questions reward that interpretation.
2. What is the main contrast in the talk?
→ The two key terms are introduced as a pair, then contrasted on mental fatigue. That pair IS the main idea.
Strategy: For academic talks, listen for the "two-term move" — when a speaker introduces a paired contrast (X vs. Y), that contrast is almost always the question target.

Writing I · Build a Sentence

You see one intact sentence followed by a sentence with words in scrambled order. The two sentences form an exchange. Reorder the words to make a grammatical reply that fits the context.

ETS sample task
Intact sentence: What was the highlight of your trip?
Build the reply:
The ___ ___ ___ ___ ___ ___ fantastic.
Sample answer: The tour guides who showed us around the old city were fantastic.
Note: "were" — plural subject (guides), not "was". "Was" is a distractor.
Strategy: Build a Sentence tests sentence-level grammar under time pressure. Find the subject first → match the verb (singular/plural) → place the relative clause "who/which" between the noun and verb.

Writing III · Write for an Academic Discussion

Online class forum: a professor opens a topic and asks a question; two students reply with different positions; you add your own substantial response. 10 minutes · at least 100 words.

ETS sample task · volunteerism
Dr. Achebe — professor

Volunteerism refers to the act of offering your time and service without financial compensation to benefit a community, organization, or cause. While many people volunteer mainly to help others, some institutions have mandatory volunteer programs. High schools are one example, where students may be required to complete a certain number of volunteer hours to graduate. What do you think? Should high school students be required to do volunteer work? Why or why not?

Claire — student

Yes, I think high schools should require volunteer hours because it helps students build a sense of civic responsibility. Many teenagers don't naturally think about helping others, and this requirement can introduce them to the idea that their time and effort can make a real difference in the lives of others.

Andrew — student

I don't think volunteer hours should be required because many students already have limited free time. Some have part-time jobs or take care of younger siblings after school. Adding a mandatory volunteer requirement could create extra stress and make it harder for those students to balance their existing responsibilities.

Target: 100+ words · take a position · address Claire or Andrew · give a concrete examplewords: 0
While I get Andrew's point about limited free time, I think it's good to have mandatory volunteer hours at high schools. Volunteering teaches students empathy and community service, helping them grow into responsible adults. Schools can give flexible options that fit students' schedules, like weekend or summer volunteering. What's more, volunteering can be a break from academic pressure, allowing students to engage in meaningful activities outside school. For example, last summer I volunteered to help create a community garden and the experience was both fulfilling and educational. I helped beautify the neighborhood and in the process of doing so, I learned how to grow vegetables and made some new friends. The potential benefits of volunteering clearly outweigh the time-constraints involved.
Strategy: The shape that scores 5: acknowledge classmate → take position → reason 1 → reason 2 → specific example → wrap-up. Examiners count the "specific example" as a separate scoring criterion.

Speaking II · Take an Interview

A simulated interview with a pre-recorded researcher. Four questions on factual/personal experience and opinion. 45 seconds per answer.

ETS sample task · research study on urban life

"You have agreed to take part in a research study about urban life. You will have a short online interview with a researcher. The researcher will ask you some questions."

▶ Researcher · 4 questions
Q1 · personal experience

Think back to the last time you visited a city in your country — a city that you didn't live in. Why did you travel to that city? What did you like about that city?

Q2 · self-reflection

Cities affect people in different ways. Some people find cities dynamic and exciting. Others find that cities are overwhelming and drain them of energy. What kind of reaction do you have to cities? Why do you think you react in this way?

Q3 · opinion

Some people believe that those who live in cities lead more interesting lives. They would argue, for example, that people who live in cities have more access to professional opportunities and interesting leisure activities. Do you agree that people who live in cities lead more interesting lives? Why or why not?

Q4 · policy opinion

For some time now, researchers have been interested in whether green spaces, such as parks, make people who live in cities happier. Do you think that city governments should create more parks in urban areas to promote a general sense of happiness and life satisfaction? Why or why not?

Target: ~45 sec per answer · 4 short paragraphs · clear opinion + 1 example eachwords: 0
Q1. The last time I visited a city was Delhi. I went there to visit my friend who is living there. I really liked the historical places, like Red Fort and the Qutub Minar. There was a lot of cultural activities going on. The food was also amazing, especially the street food. I enjoyed the busy atmosphere and the way it mixes the old and the new buildings. Q2. I usually feel excited and kind of energized in cities. I think it's because there's always something happening, and there's so many people around. It makes me feel like I'm part of a big community. I also love the variety of things to do and see. Sometimes it gets a bit tiring, but overall, I like the dynamic environment. Q3. I agree that people in cities live more interesting lives. There's more access to professional opportunities and fun activities. You can meet many different kinds of people and learn a lot of new things. The city has cultural events, nice restaurants, and different kinds of places to visit. I think it makes life more exciting. Q4. I think city governments should really try to create more parks. In a busy city, it's really helpful to have these quiet areas to get away from the noise and stress. I think it definitely helps improve people's overall well-being and just makes the city better.

Scoring Guides · Writing (0 – 5)

Both Write an Email and Write for an Academic Discussion are scored 0–5 with parallel rubrics. Here's what the examiner is looking for.

Write an Email

5
Fully successful

Effective, clearly expressed, consistent facility with language.

  • Elaboration that effectively supports the communicative purpose
  • Effective syntactic variety and precise, idiomatic word choice
  • Appropriate social conventions (politeness, register, request formulation)
  • Almost no lexical or grammatical errors
4
Generally successful

Mostly effective and easily understood. Adequate language facility.

  • Adequate elaboration; mostly appropriate social conventions
  • Syntactic variety and appropriate word choice
  • Few errors
3
Partially successful

Generally accomplishes the task. Limitations may prevent parts of the message from being fully clear.

  • Partial elaboration; moderate range of syntax and vocabulary
  • Some noticeable errors in structure, word forms or social conventions
2
Mostly unsuccessful

Attempts the task but is mostly ineffective. Message limited or hard to interpret.

  • Limited or irrelevant elaboration; limited range of syntax/vocabulary
  • Accumulation of errors
1
Unsuccessful

Ineffective attempt; message may be unintelligible.

  • Very little elaboration · telegraphic language · serious frequent errors
  • Minimal original language
0
No score

Blank · rejects the topic · not in English · entirely copied from the prompt · arbitrary keystrokes.

Write for an Academic Discussion

Same 0–5 banding. A band-5 response shows relevant, well-elaborated explanations, exemplifications or details · effective syntactic variety · precise, idiomatic word choice. The discussion is judged on contribution to the forum, not on agreement with the professor.

Why this matters: Both Writing rubrics reward elaboration — they specifically score whether your supporting examples and reasoning are relevant and developed, not whether you write more words. Two strong examples beat four shallow ones.

Past Tenses · targeted drill — три зоны направленной отработки

Эта секция собрана по результатам прошедшей самостоятельной работы. Три маркерные конструкции, в которых чаще всего возникают ошибки в Past, плюс мини-карточки неправильных глаголов. Цель — автоматизация в продуктивной речи.

① Маркер «ago» — обязательное Past Simple

Наречие ago однозначно требует Past Simple. Сочетание ago + Present Perfect грамматически некорректно.

1. The report ___ five minutes ago.
→ «ago» + V2 → Past Simple (arrived).
2. They ___ the contract two hours ago.
→ «ago» → Past Simple (signed).
3. The CFO ___ to Singapore three weeks ago.
→ «ago» → Past Simple (flew).
4. We ___ the email a minute ago.
→ «ago» → Past Simple (sent).
5. I ___ to that conference six months ago.
→ «ago» → Past Simple (went).
6. Our team ___ that pilot project two years ago.
→ «ago» → Past Simple (launched).
7. The bank ___ that policy a few months ago.
→ «ago» → Past Simple (changed).
8. The client ___ us this requirement ten minutes ago.
→ «ago» → Past Simple (told).

② Конструкция «While + V-ing» — Past Continuous (фон) + Past Simple (прерывание)

Союз while требует Past Continuous (was / were + V-ing) для длительного фонового действия. Прерывающее действие — Past Simple.

1. While I ___ to the office, my phone rang.
→ While + V-ing → Past Continuous (was walking).
2. While she ___ the labelling, the system crashed.
→ Past Continuous (was checking).
3. While we ___ the report, the manager came in.
→ Past Continuous фон (were discussing) + Past Simple прерывание (came).
4. While he ___ for the bus, it started to rain.
→ Past Continuous (was waiting).
5. While the team ___ the presentation, the projector died.
→ Past Continuous (was preparing) — длительный фон.
6. While I ___ at that company, I learned a lot about logistics.
→ While + V-ing → Past Continuous (was working).
7. While they ___ the documents, I made some coffee.
→ Past Continuous (were reviewing).
8. While my colleague ___ the call, I took the minutes.
→ Past Continuous (was leading) — фон.

③ Past Simple при описании завершённого периода («когда я был…»)

Если контекст описывает завершённый отрезок прошлого («когда я был студентом», «в школе я…»), используется Past Simple, а не Present.

1. When I was a student, I ___ as a tutor on weekends.
→ «When I was…» → Past Simple (worked).
2. At university we ___ a lot of group projects.
→ Завершённый период (at university) → Past Simple (did).
3. In my first job I ___ in logistics for two years.
→ «In my first job» — закрытый период → Past Simple (worked).
4. When I lived in St Petersburg, I ___ at a startup.
→ «When I lived…» — закрытый период → Past Simple (worked).
5. In 2022 we ___ in three different cities.
→ Точный год «in 2022» → Past Simple (lived).
6. During my MBA, I ___ a lot about negotiation.
→ «During my MBA» — закрытый период → Past Simple (learned).

⑤ Said vs Told — grammar trap

told требует прямое дополнение (told me / told him); said используется без дополнения или с to (said to me, said it).

1. He ___ me he was busy.
→ есть прямое дополнение «me» → told.
2. She ___ that the meeting was postponed.
→ нет дополнения, перед «that» → said.
3. The manager ___ us to wait outside.
→ есть дополнение «us» → told.
4. «I'll be late», he ___ to me on the phone.
→ конструкция «said to + person» → said.
5. My tutor ___ me the truth about the exam.
→ есть «me» → told.
6. She ___ nothing during the meeting.
→ дополнения нет → said.

⑥ -ing forms · gerund / participle / continuous

Три функции -ing: (a) gerund — V-ing работает как существительное (Reading is fun); (b) participle — V-ing в роли причастия (the man reading the book); (c) continuous — V-ing в составе времени (is reading).

1. «Reading academic texts is hard.» — функция -ing?
→ Reading = подлежащее, существительное → gerund.
2. «He is reading the brief.» — функция -ing?
→ is + V-ing → Present Continuous.
3. «The man reading the report is my boss.» — функция -ing?
→ reading = определение к «the man» → participle.
4. «I enjoy learning new languages.» — функция -ing?
→ после enjoy всегда gerund → gerund.
5. «Walking down the street, I saw an old friend.» — функция -ing?
→ обстоятельство одновременного действия → participle.
6. «She was preparing the presentation when I called.» — функция «preparing»?
→ was + V-ing → Past Continuous.

⑦ Past Tenses · определи время

Шесть предложений в смешанном порядке. Определи: Past Simple · Past Continuous · Past Perfect.

1. «Last year I attended an IELTS prep course.»
→ «last year» + V2 → Past Simple.
2. «At 9 a.m. yesterday I was checking the inventory.»
→ «at 9 a.m. yesterday» + was V-ing → Past Continuous.
3. «By Friday we had completed the whole project.»
→ «by Friday» + had V3 → Past Perfect.
4. «I was reading the contract when the phone rang.»
→ «was reading» — фоновое действие → Past Continuous.
5. «Before I joined the company, I had worked in marketing for 3 years.»
→ «had worked» — действие до другого момента в прошлом → Past Perfect.
6. «Yesterday morning I sent three follow-up emails.»
→ «yesterday morning» + V2 → Past Simple.
