Grammar Recap · 5 tenses in the hotel

Same five tenses we've been drilling — now in a hotel scene. Choose the right form. One try per question.

1. Past Simple. I ___ at the hotel yesterday.

2. Present Perfect. I ___ room service twice today.

3. Present Continuous. The staff ___ my room right now.

4. Future Simple. I ___ tomorrow morning at 8.

5. Present Perfect Continuous. I ___ for room service since noon.

6. Right now, the receptionist ___ in a new guest.

7. I ___ at this hotel three times last year.

8. They ___ room service 10 minutes ago.

🧩 Put «stay» in the correct tense

Present Perfect
I have stayed at that hotel several times.
multiple experiences up to now.
Past Simple
Last summer, I stayed at a beach resort.
finished action, finished time.
Present Continuous
I am staying with friends this week.
temporary, around now.
Future Simple
Next month, I will stay in a cabin.
plan or promise.
Present Perfect Cont.
I have been staying here for a week.
action started in the past and still going.

Role-Play Lab · three scenarios

Three real hotel situations. Read your role only, then play it out loud with your partner or teacher.

🎭 Scenario A · Standard check-in (late evening)
Role A · Guest
  • You've just arrived late. You have a reservation under your name.
  • Confirm booking, ask about breakfast, Wi-Fi, late check-out, luggage storage and local sights.
  • Ask for a wake-up call at 7 a.m.
  • Use: «I'd like to check in.» · «I have a reservation under …» · «Could I have a wake-up call?»
Role B · Receptionist
  • Welcome the guest, confirm booking, hand out the key card.
  • Breakfast 7–10, Wi-Fi complimentary, check-out at 11 (late until 12 for a small fee).
  • Offer to store luggage after check-out. Recommend two local sights.
  • Confirm the wake-up call and give a direct number to reception.
⚠️ Scenario B · Complaint about poor service
Role A · Dissatisfied guest
  • The room hasn't been cleaned properly, staff were rude, dinner was cold.
  • Ask to speak to a manager. Stay polite but firm.
  • Ask for a real solution — not just an apology.
  • Use: «I'm afraid I'm not satisfied with…» · «I'd like to file a complaint.» · «Is there a way to make up for this?»
Role B · Hotel manager
  • Apologise. Listen without interrupting. Repeat the guest's issue back.
  • Offer two options: room upgrade + free dinner tomorrow, or 30 % off the bill.
  • Give your direct extension. Send someone to re-clean the room within 20 minutes.
  • Do not over-promise — stay calm and specific.
🧯 Scenario C · Report a problem in the room
Role A · Guest
  • You've just checked in — problems: AC doesn't work, tap leaking, no towels, remote missing.
  • Call reception. Report each issue clearly, one by one.
  • Ask how soon it can be fixed. Be firm but polite.
  • Use: «There seems to be a problem with…» · «Could you send someone to fix it?» · «How soon can it be resolved?»
Role B · Maintenance / receptionist
  • Confirm each issue back to the guest. Ask for the room number.
  • Send fresh towels + remote within 10 minutes.
  • Maintenance team will look at the AC and tap in the next 30 minutes.
  • Offer a complimentary drink for the inconvenience.
Drill order: read your role silently → play out loud once slowly → switch roles → play again fluently without script. Time per scenario: ~5 minutes.

Useful Tips · 8 hotel lifehacks

Read each tip silently, then re-read it aloud in your own words.

  • Compare the prices for hotel rooms on different websites before you book.
  • Choose hotels with good reviews — not just star ratings.
  • Ask for a corner room if you want a larger space at the same price.
  • Ask for a room with a special view when you check in.
  • Sign up for a hotel's loyalty programme for free upgrades and late check-out.
  • Mention any special occasion (birthday, anniversary, honeymoon) — hotels often add small extras.
  • Check if your credit card has hotel rewards.
  • Tell the administrator immediately if there are any problems — early is easier to fix.
Speaking drill: pick 3 tips and turn them into advice for a friend: «You should …», «Make sure to …», «Don't forget to …».
