05

Quick Read · The Missing Suitcase

A 90-second story with new vocab in context. Read silently first, then aloud once.

It was almost midnight when Anna walked into the baggage claim. The carousel turned and turned. Black suitcases, blue suitcases — but not hers. After thirty minutes the lights above the belt went off. Anna's bag was nowhere.

She walked to the airline desk. A tired agent in a navy uniform looked up. «Boarding pass and a photo of your bag, please.» Anna handed over her phone. The agent typed slowly. «It's still in Munich. We will deliver it to your hotel by tomorrow afternoon. Here is a small amenity kit — toothbrush, t-shirt, a few basics.»

Anna took a taxi to her hotel. The lobby was quiet. The night clerk smiled. «No luggage tonight?» Anna shook her head. «It's on a different flight. They'll drop it by tomorrow.» The clerk handed her a key card and a bottle of water. «Welcome anyway.»

Comprehension · say aloud: 1) Where did Anna look for her bag first? 2) What two things did the airline agent give her? 3) When will the suitcase arrive? 4) How did the night clerk react? — Full sentence each, in your own words.
06

Smart Planning · Hotel check-in · 8 steps

From the lobby door to the elevator. Eight steps, eight short sentences. Use the prompts to build a natural check-in monologue.

  1. Reservation — «I have a reservation under [name].»
  2. ID & passport — «Here's my passport and the booking confirmation.»
  3. Key card — «Could I get an extra key card?»
  4. Wi-Fi — «What's the Wi-Fi password? Is it free?»
  5. Breakfast hours — «What time does breakfast start? Where is the dining room?»
  6. Room & luggage — «Can I have a quiet room? And can I leave my bag at reception after check-out?»
  7. Contact reception — «Should I call reception if I need anything during the night?»
  8. Thanks & goodbye — «Thanks for the help — have a good evening.»
Speaking drill: chain all 8 steps into one fluent monologue. Time yourself — aim for under 90 seconds. Then swap roles: you are the receptionist, your trainer is the guest.

Listening · Three hotel reviews

Three guests, three different nights at the Grand View. Read the transcript, hit ▶ to listen, then answer the comprehension questions out loud.

Review 01 · Maya · solo traveller

«Arrived after midnight. The night clerk was friendly even though I was clearly tired. Key card worked first try. Room was small but quiet — exactly what I needed for a layover. Breakfast at six in the morning was a lifesaver. Would stay again on a quick stop.»

Review 02 · Daniel · business trip

«Good location near the station, but Wi-Fi was painfully slow for video calls. Reception offered a fix and moved me to a higher floor. The new room had a much better signal. Coffee in the lobby was decent. Three stars — would book again only if work needs a quick base.»

Review 03 · Sophie & partner · weekend break

«We booked a double with a view. The view was real — old town rooftops and a slice of the river. The bed was comfortable but the bathroom needed a deep clean. We told the manager and she offered a free breakfast upgrade, which was very kind. Mixed feelings, but the small touches saved it.»

11

Role-Play Lab · three scenarios

Three travel role-plays — A and B sides. Read your role only. Then play it out loud — first slowly, then fluently.

Scenario A · Lost luggage at baggage claim
Role A · Traveller
  • You arrived from Munich on flight LH 9211, 22:40.
  • Your black suitcase is missing. There is a yellow ribbon on the handle.
  • Inside: clothes for a 5-day trip, a small gift bag, no valuables.
  • Ask the airline desk to file a report and deliver it to your hotel: Grand View, room booked under your name.
Role B · Airline agent
  • Greet the passenger politely. Ask for boarding pass and a photo of the bag.
  • Say the bag is most likely still in Munich and will arrive on the next flight.
  • Offer an amenity kit (toothbrush, t-shirt). Confirm delivery to the hotel by tomorrow afternoon.
  • Give a tracking reference and the local 24-hour number.
Scenario B · Hotel check-in with overbooking
Role A · Guest
  • You booked a double room, two nights, paid in advance.
  • You have a confirmation email on your phone.
  • You need a quiet room — early flight tomorrow morning.
  • Ask for a real solution: another room of the same category, or a partner hotel nearby with transfer.
Role B · Receptionist
  • Apologise — the hotel is overbooked tonight, only one twin room is left.
  • Offer two options: upgrade to a suite at no charge, or a free transfer to a 4-star partner hotel 10 minutes away.
  • Promise to come back tomorrow night in the original room category, no extra charge.
  • Stay polite, do not over-promise. Give your name and a direct number.
Scenario C · Asking for directions in a foreign city · callback to Light Bright 1
Role A · Tourist
  • You need to get from the train station to the National Museum on foot.
  • You have 40 minutes before it closes. Phone battery is low.
  • Ask a local: best route, how long it takes, any landmarks to look for.
  • Politely confirm the answer in your own words before saying thanks.
Role B · Local
  • The museum is a 15-minute walk. Easy route.
  • Go straight on the main street, pass two traffic lights, turn left at the cathedral square.
  • The museum is on the corner, opposite a yellow bookshop.
  • Warn: a short street between the lights is steep — comfortable shoes help.
Drill order: read your role silently → play it out loud once slowly → switch roles → play the second time fluently with no script. Time per scenario: ~5 minutes.

Phrasal Verbs of Travel · 8 + drills

Eight phrasal verbs of travel — same set as Light Bright 1, used again in new airport / hotel sentences. Tap ▶ on any verb to hear it. Then read the example aloud.

set off
to begin a journey

check in
to register
I'll check in online and then drop my bag at the desk.
take off
to leave the ground
The plane takes off at half past nine.
get away

look around
to explore a place

15

Mini-debate · Hotel vs Airbnb

Two sides, 60 seconds each. Pick a side, then argue. Then switch.

Side A · Hotel
  • Reception 24/7 — instant help if luggage is lost or anything goes wrong.
  • Breakfast included; daily housekeeping.
  • Predictable: the room you booked is the room you get.
  • Safer for short trips and late arrivals.
Side B · Airbnb
  • Feels like home — kitchen, washing machine, more space.
  • Local neighbourhoods, not tourist strips.
  • Better value for stays longer than 3 nights.
  • Hosts give real travel tips you won't find in a guidebook.
Drill: Side A speaks first (60 sec), then Side B (60 sec). Then both summarise the other side in one sentence — to prove you listened. Closing: pick a winner for tonight and one phrase you'd say to a friend who's choosing.
