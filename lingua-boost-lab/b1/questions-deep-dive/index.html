B.1 Yes / No questions · auxiliary + subject + verb

The very shape of an English question: an auxiliary in front of the subject. Which auxiliary → depends on the tense.

  • Do / Does — Present Simple (main verb work / go / like)
  • Did — Past Simple (main verb in bare form: work, not worked)
  • Is / Am / Are — Present Continuous (main verb + -ing) & to-be states
  • Was / Were — Past Continuous / to-be states in the past
  • Have / Has — Present Perfect (main verb V3: been / seen / done)
  • Will — Future (main verb in bare form)
  • Are you going to — planned future / evidence
Do you like flying? · Did you book the hotel? · Is the plane boarding? · Have you been to Prague? · Will you upgrade your seat?
Правило: аукс перед подлежащим. Главный глагол потом. Никаких «Do you liked?» и «Are you booked?».
Not: «You like flying?» (без Do) — так спрашивают только очень informal. На B1/B2 — держим Do/Does/Did в начале.

B.2 Wh-questions · the seven basics + How-family

Wh-word comes first, then the same Yes/No frame (auxiliary + subject + verb).

  • What — thing / activity (What did you order?)
  • Where — place (Where is your hotel?)
  • When — time (When does the flight land?)
  • Who — person (Who booked the tickets?)
  • Whose — belonging (Whose suitcase is this?)
  • Which — choice from a limited set (Which terminal, 1 or 2?)
  • Why — reason (Why is the flight delayed?)

How-family — measure / manner:

  • How — way / manner (How was the trip?)
  • How much — uncountable / price (How much is a taxi?)
  • How many — countable (How many bags do you have?)
  • How often — frequency (How often do you fly?)
  • How long — duration (How long is the flight?)
  • How far — distance (How far is the hotel from the airport?)
What time do you land? · Where are you staying? · How long is the layover?
Формула: Wh + Do/Does/Did/Is/Are/Have/Will + подлежащее + глагол.

B.3 Subject vs Object questions · when to drop the auxiliary

If the Wh-word IS the subject — no auxiliary, no inversion. Just Wh + verb.

  • Object question: Who did you meet? → I met Anna. (Anna = object)
  • Subject question: Who met you? → Anna met me. (Anna = subject)

Same pattern with What, Which, How many:

What happened? · Who called you? · How many people booked this tour? · Which flight costs the least?
Правило: Wh-слово = подлежащее → вспомогательный НЕ нужен. Wh-слово = дополнение → нужен Do/Does/Did.
Test yourself: if the answer names a doer (Anna called me), the question is Subject. If the answer names a receiver (I called Anna), the question is Object.

A.1 Alternative questions · X or Y

Same frame as a Yes/No question, but with or offering two options. Intonation: rise on X, fall on Y.

Would you like tea ↗ or coffee ↘? · Are we flying morning ↗ or evening ↘? · Do you prefer the window ↗ or the aisle ↘?
Строим как Yes/No вопрос, но добавляем «or Y». Голос: ↗ на первое слово, ↘ на второе.
Answer is NOT «Yes» / «No». The listener must pick one: "Tea, please." Not «Yes, please» — that would mean neither.

A.2 Tag questions · positive → negative, negative → positive

A tag question is a mini-question at the end of a statement, asking for confirmation. The auxiliary in the tag mirrors the statement, but flips polarity.

  • Positive → negative tag: You are Katya, aren't you?
  • Negative → positive tag: The flight isn't full, is it?

Which auxiliary to pick? Repeat the auxiliary of the statement:

  • Present Simple + main verb → use do/does/don't/doesn't — You like flying, don't you?
  • Past Simple + main verb → use did/didn't — They booked the tour, didn't they?
  • Any modal / auxiliary → repeat it — You can drive, can't you? / She has already checked in, hasn't she?
You booked the hotel, didn't you? · The flight isn't full, is it? · Katya can speak German, can't she?
Хвост зеркалит утверждение: аукс тот же, знак наоборот. Интонация вниз ↘ = «жду подтверждения». Вверх ↗ = «реально спрашиваю».
Two curious exceptions: «I am… → aren't I?» (not amn't I). «Let's… → shall we?»

A.3 The Polite Ladder · 5 steps of «could you please»

Native English builds politeness in visible steps. Learn the ladder — climb it depending on who you're talking to and how much you're asking.

  1. Direct (bare) · casual — «Pass the salt.» / «Open the window.» → for family and close friends only.
  2. Can I / Can you · neutral — «Can I have a coffee? / Can you help me?» — everyday tone with strangers.
  3. Could I / Could you · polite — «Could I have a coffee? / Could you help me?» — most hotels, cafés, airports.
  4. May I · formal — «May I have your passport, please?» — official contexts, older register.
  5. Would you mind + -ing / I was wondering if… · very polite / careful — «Would you mind moving your bag? / I was wondering if you could recommend a good restaurant.»
Casual: Give me your ticket. · Neutral: Can I see your ticket? · Polite: Could I see your ticket, please? · Formal: May I see your ticket? · Very polite: Would you mind showing me your ticket?
Формула: чем выше уровень — тем менее прямо звучит. Отель / кафе / аэропорт → минимум Could I / Could you.
«Would you mind + -ing» → форма герундий, НЕ инфинитив: «Would you mind opening the window?», не «Would you mind to open…».

A.4 Indirect questions · wrap a question inside a polite frame

Direct: Where is the bus stop? → Indirect: Do you know where the bus stop is?

What changes:

  • Word order switches back to subject + verb (no more auxiliary inversion inside).
  • For Yes/No questions, use if or whether: Do you know if the shop is open?
  • «Do you know», «Could you tell me», «I was wondering», «I'd like to know», «Have you any idea…» — all valid openers.
Direct: Where is the metro? → Indirect: Could you tell me where the metro is?
Direct: Does the shop take cash? → Indirect: Do you know if the shop takes cash?
Внутри косвенного вопроса — обычный порядок как в утверждении. Никаких «is the metro», «does the shop take» после «где / если».
Very common mistake: «Could you tell me where is the metro?» — no. Correct: «Could you tell me where the metro is?»

WO.2 Frequency adverbs · где стоят слова частотности

These are special wagons — they NEVER sit in the SVOMPT chain. They have their own rule.

  • BEFORE the main verb: Nelly often comes home late. · We all live in Moscow. · They never ride home on a bike.
  • AFTER to be: She is always on time. · They are never at home on Sundays. · Bill is also ill.
  • BETWEEN modal/aux and verb: You can also use the app. · She has already called.

The full family:

always · usually · often · sometimes · rarely · seldom · never · already · still · just · both · also · all · ever
Слова частотности сидят вплотную к главному глаголу: перед смысловым, но после to be / после первого вспомогательного.
Common mistake: «She always is on time.» → wrong. «She is always on time.» → correct. To be = always after.

R.1 Reading · «Two travellers, two styles»

Read the article twice: first for gist (who is who?), then for detail. Only then answer the 10 mixed questions below — Yes/No · Wh · Subject/Object · Alternative · Tag · Indirect.

  • Gist reading: speed up. Skip words you don't know. Grab the shape: Who is Lena? Who is Marco? What happened?
  • Detail reading: slow down. Circle numbers, names, dates: how many countries? which trip? what changed?
  • Watch the question type before you answer — a Yes/No question wants a yes/no answer, a Wh-question wants a fact, an Indirect question sneaks the answer inside politeness.
Lena's own words: «I want to feel the city, not to collect it.» — a whole personality in ten words. Notice how a good writer packs identity into a single quote.
Совет: сперва пробежать глазами (gist), потом вернуться и подметить цифры и имена (detail). Читать один раз и сразу отвечать — почти всегда ловушка на B1.

R.1.a Article · 220 words · B1

Two travellers, two styles

Lena and Marco met at a hostel in Rome three years ago. Lena is a slow traveller: she books one place, stays for two weeks, learns the names of the café owners on her street, and rarely takes photos. «I want to feel the city, not to collect it,» she says.

Marco is the opposite. He is a checklist traveller: seven countries a month, two hundred photos a day, and a spreadsheet where he ranks every restaurant. He has already visited forty-three countries; Lena has visited eleven.

Last summer they decided to travel together to Iceland. It was a disaster and a revelation at the same time. On day one, Marco wanted to drive around the whole ring road; Lena wanted to sit by a single glacier and read. On day three, they compromised: they moved once every two days and cooked their own meals in each hostel.

By the end of the trip, Lena had taken more photos than usual, and Marco had learnt three Icelandic words. «I think we changed each other a little,» Lena says. «Not enough to swap styles — but enough to understand.»

They're planning a second trip. This time, Georgia. Marco is already reading about Tbilisi. Lena is looking for a small village with a river.

W.1 Your growth points · fix the five most common question mistakes

Every B1 learner hits the same five walls with questions. Learn to notice them — you'll edit yourself in real time.

  1. Auxiliary missing · «You like flying?» → Do you like flying?
  2. Auxiliary doubled · «Did you liked it?» → Did you like it?
  3. Word order inside indirect · «Could you tell me where is the metro?» → Could you tell me where the metro is?
  4. Subject question · extra Do/Does · «Who did call you?» → Who called you?
  5. Tag polarity · «You are Katya, are you?» → You are Katya, aren't you?
Rule of thumb: after Do/Does/Did → base verb. After Have/Has → V3. After Is/Are/Was/Were → -ing or adjective. Fix the auxiliary first, everything else follows.
