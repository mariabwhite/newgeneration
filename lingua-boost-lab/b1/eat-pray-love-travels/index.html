Meet Elizabeth Gilbert

Warm-up · 3 minutes · about the author + the journey

Elizabeth Gilbert is an American writer. In 2006 she published a book called Eat, Pray, Love — a personal story about her divorce, her sadness, and her decision to travel for a whole year. She went to three countries — one for each part of the title.

The idea: Italy — how to enjoy life again. India — how to be quiet with yourself. Indonesia (Bali) — how to balance both.

🇮🇹 Italy · Rome · the art of pleasure

Reading + listening + 6 vocabulary cards + 3 comprehension questions

Rome · Italy

Il dolce far niente —
the sweetness of doing nothing

🎬
Video story · a rainy afternoon in Rome
A small trattoria near Piazza Navona. Rain outside, warm bread inside. The waiter smiles and says: take your time.
🔊 Read + listen · Salli · US voice

I arrived in Rome in the middle of a rainy October. My Italian teacher had told me one phrase before I left New York — il dolce far niente. The sweetness of doing nothing. In Rome, doing nothing is not laziness. It is an art.

People sit in cafés for two hours over one small espresso. They watch, they talk, they simply exist. On my third day, I ordered pasta in a small trattoria near Piazza Navona. The waiter smiled and said, take your time. I understood then — I had forgotten what pleasure felt like.

✅ Check yourself · 3 questions

Click the correct answer · explanation appears after

1 What does il dolce far niente mean?
→ Literally «the sweet doing-nothing». For Romans it's not laziness — it's a life philosophy: sitting, watching, existing without a task.
2 How long do people typically sit in a Roman café over one espresso?
→ Two hours over one small espresso — that's the Italian rhythm. In the text: «People sit in cafés for two hours over one small espresso.»
3 What had the narrator forgotten before she came to Rome?
→ «I had forgotten what pleasure felt like.» Rome reminded her — through food, slowness, and the waiter's «take your time».
02

🇮🇳 India · Ashram · the art of silence

Reading + listening + 6 vocabulary cards + 3 comprehension questions

Ashram · India

Silence at 4 in the morning

🎬
Video story · ashram at dawn
A stone courtyard before sunrise. Two hundred people sit cross-legged. Only breathing — and one bell, once every hour.
🔊 Read + listen · Kimberly · US voice

After Italy came India. And after pasta came the ashram. Meditation began at four in the morning. My knees hurt. My mind screamed. I wanted to run out of the hall and never come back.

But the teacher told me one thing — do not fight your thoughts. Watch them like clouds. Let them pass. On the seventh day, something quiet happened inside me. A small silence. Not empty — full. Like the moment after a bell has finished ringing.

✅ Check yourself · 3 questions

Click the correct answer · explanation appears after

1 At what time did meditation begin at the ashram?
→ «Meditation began at four in the morning.» That was the daily rule — before sunrise, before the world starts asking anything from you.
2 What did the teacher tell her to do with her thoughts?
→ Not fight — watch. The idea is to observe, not to argue. Thoughts pass on their own if you don't grab them.
3 What kind of silence did she feel on the seventh day?
→ Not empty — full. A silence with presence in it. That image of the bell is the key — the sound is gone but something still resonates.
03

🇮🇩 Indonesia · Bali · the art of balance

Reading + listening + 6 vocabulary cards + 3 comprehension questions

Bali · Indonesia

The medicine man and the balance he taught

🎬
Video story · a village in Ubud
Green terraces stepping down the hill. In a small wooden house sits an old man with a soft laugh. He has been reading palms for sixty years.
🔊 Read + listen · Joanna · US voice

Bali was different. Italy had taught me pleasure. India had taught me silence. Bali asked me to bring both into one life. I met an old medicine man in a village near Ubud. His name was Ketut. He drew a picture for me — a person with four legs and a smiling face, standing on a small green earth.

To find balance, he said, you must keep your feet on the ground and your heart open. Not too much thinking. Not too much feeling. Just watch, and smile. Bali does the rest.

✅ Check yourself · 3 questions

Click the correct answer · explanation appears after

1 What lesson did Bali offer her?
→ Bali is the synthesis — Italy + India in one place. Not either/or but both/and. That's the whole point of the third journey.
2 Who did she meet in Ubud?
→ Ketut Liyer — a real person from Gilbert's memoir. In the text: «I met an old medicine man in a village near Ubud. His name was Ketut.»
3 According to Ketut, what does balance require?
→ Grounded, open, watching. Balance = middle way. Not the intensity of the ashram, not the abandon of Rome — the still centre between them.

Discussion & Check

🎙 Discussion · 5 open questions
Q1Gilbert calls Italy the art of pleasure, India the art of silence, Bali the art of balance. Which of the three arts do you feel you need the most right now — and why?
Q2The Italians have a phrase «il dolce far niente» Italy. In your culture, is doing nothing seen as art or as laziness? Give one example from your family or country.
Q3At the ashram Gilbert had to surrender her ideas about how meditation should feel India. When did you last have to surrender an idea about how life «should» be? What happened after?
Q4Ketut Liyer, the Balinese medicine man, says balance is not «half-half» but knowing when to give more to one side Bali. Where in your life would «real balance» look unbalanced from outside?
Q5Gilbert travelled for a whole year to find herself. Do you think a person needs to leave to change — or can real change happen without going anywhere?
✅ True / False · 8 statements about the story
1ItalyGilbert travels to Rome mainly to study Italian language and food.
True. Rome is her chapter of pleasure: language, pasta, unhurried meals — she calls it «learning to be an Italian» and reclaims her appetite.
2Italy«Dolce far niente» means «sweet homework» in Italian.
False. It means «the sweetness of doing nothing» — pleasure in idleness itself, not in any task done.
3IndiaAt the ashram she wakes up at 4 a.m. for meditation.
True. Pre-dawn silence is a core practice of the ashram — Gilbert struggles with it before she learns to «sit in it».
4IndiaMeditation at the ashram comes to her easily from the first week.
False. The opposite — her mind is loud and rebellious. She only begins to break through after weeks of discipline and one intense night on the roof.
5BaliKetut Liyer, the medicine man, predicted she would return to Bali before she actually did.
True. On her first short visit to Bali he reads her palm and tells her she will come back and stay with him. That prediction is the reason she chooses Bali as the third stop.
6BaliIn Bali she launches a business selling essential oils.
False. In Bali she helps raise money to build a house for Wayan, a local healer and single mother. No essential-oils business.
7ItalyIndiaBaliThe whole journey is exactly one year long — four months in each country.
True. That's the structural spine of the book — 12 months, split in three, one lesson per stop.
8ItalyIndiaBaliGilbert's main goal is to teach English in each of the three countries.
False. The journey is not about work — it's a personal search after divorce. She is learning, meditating, healing, not teaching.
