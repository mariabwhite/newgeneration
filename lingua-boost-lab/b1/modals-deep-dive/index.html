Modals 1 · what you can do · what's allowed · what's a good idea

Can · Could · May · Should · Had better

What you CAN do · ability

FormExample
can (present)I can ride a bike with no hands. Don't try it at home.
could (past)My grandmother could recite long poems by heart.
be able to (any tense)After ten months of yoga, she was able to touch her toes.
The tricky one: for one specific success in the past, use was/were able to, NOT could. "She tried and tried, and finally she was able to fix the bike" — never could here.

What's ALLOWED · permission

FormExample
can (informal · friends)Can I borrow your charger? Mine just died.
could (polite · adults)Could I have a quick word after the lesson?
may (formal · school, work)May I be excused, please? I don't feel well.
Reading the room: the colder the relationship, the further down the list you go. can = mate. could = anyone you respect. may = exam, head teacher, hotel reception.

What's a GOOD IDEA · advice

FormExample
shouldIf you're cold, you should put on a jumper.
ought to (a touch stronger)We ought to tell her — she'll find out anyway.
had better ('d better · urgent)You 'd better text her now, or she'll be worried sick.
Watch out: had better sounds like normal advice but it actually carries a warning. "You'd better study" = "or else something bad will happen". Don't use it lightly with friends, they'll feel pressured.

Modals 2 · rules · guesses · what might happen

Must · Have to · Mustn't · Should · Could · May · Might

RULES · obligation

FormExample
must (strong, inner)I must finish this essay before Friday — no excuses.
have to (outside rule)My little sister has to take her medicine three times a day.
need to (neutral)You need to sign here, please.
mustn't (forbidden)Shh — you mustn't tell anyone, it's a surprise!
had to (past)Last summer I had to babysit every weekend.
The key difference: must = it comes from inside you. have to = it comes from outside (school, parents, the law). "I must drink more water" (my own decision). "I have to wear a uniform" (school rule).

NO RULES · no obligation

FormExample
don't have toIt's a free day — you don't have to wear a uniform.
needn't · don't need toYou needn't worry, I'll handle everything.
The big trap: mustn't ≠ don't have to. "You mustn't open that door" = it's forbidden. "You don't have to open that door" = it's your choice. Mixing these up changes the whole meaning.

GUESSES · probability (now & future)

FormExample
should · ought to (likely)The package was sent on Monday — it should arrive any minute.
must (sure · evidence)Her bag is here — she must still be in the building somewhere.
can't · couldn't (impossible)He's in Spain — that can't be him at the supermarket.

MAYBE · possibility

FormExample
could · may · mightI don't know what's in the box — it could / may / might be a present from gran.

Modals · a quiet superpower

In English, modals are how you say HOW you feel about a fact. "She is at the gym" is a statement. "She might be at the gym" is a guess. "She must be at the gym" is a confident bet. "She can't be at the gym" is the rejection of a wrong idea. Same fact — three different worlds. Once you hear that, you stop translating from Russian. You start thinking in modals.
