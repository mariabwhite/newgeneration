Block 2 · Passive

Passive Voice — all tenses + modals

Reference · Passive in every tense

Formula: be (in the right tense) + V3 (past participle). Agent optional: by + agent. Use passive when the doer is unknown, obvious, or unimportant.

TenseActivePassive formulaPassive example
Present SimpleThey make cars in Germany.am/is/are + V3Cars are made in Germany.
Present ContinuousThey are building a new hospital.am/is/are being + V3A new hospital is being built.
Present PerfectThey have opened the museum.have/has been + V3The museum has been opened.
Past SimplePeter founded the city in 1703.was/were + V3The city was founded in 1703.
Past ContinuousThey were repairing the road.was/were being + V3The road was being repaired.
Past PerfectThey had finished the palace by 1762.had been + V3The palace had been finished by 1762.
Future SimpleThey will publish the results.will be + V3The results will be published.
Modals (must / can / should)Students must submit the essay.modal + be + V3The essay must be submitted.
Perfect modalsSomeone should have told her.modal + have been + V3She should have been told.
Gerund / InfinitiveI don't like people watching me.being + V3 · to be + V3I don't like being watched. · The window needs to be repaired.

Reference · what each modal means
ModalMeaningExample
can / can'tability, permission, general possibilityShe can speak three languages.
couldpast ability, polite request, possibilityI could read at four. · Could you help me?
may / mightpossibility (less sure), permissionIt may rain. · You may leave now.

don't have tono obligation (≠ mustn't = prohibition)You don't have to come. · You mustn't smoke here.

Block 4 · will · would · used to

Past habits & Future in the Past

Reference · three forms · three meanings

used to + V — прошлая привычка или состояние, которого больше нет. would + V — повторявшееся действие в прошлом (нельзя для состояний! ✗ I would be shy). will / would — прогноз / Future in the Past.

FormUseExampleTrap
used to + Vpast habit or state, no longer trueI used to live in Kazan. · She used to be shy.✓ works with states AND actions
didn't use tonegative — no d in the baseI didn't use to like coffee.✗ didn't used to
would + Vrepeated past action (like a story)Every summer we would go to the dacha.✗ I would be shy — need used to be
will + Vprediction / decision now / offerI think it will rain. · I'll help you.— no if-clause after if
would + V (Future in the Past)reported / sequence of tensesShe said she would come.✗ said she will come
be used to + V-ingto be accustomed to (state now)I'm used to getting up early.≠ used to — different meaning
get used to + V-ingto become accustomed toShe'll get used to living abroad.✗ get used to live

Block 5 · Gerund vs Inf

V-ing or to + V?

Reference · which verb takes what

Rule: some verbs take only V-ing, some only to + V, some — both with different meanings. After a preposition — always gerund. After make / let / help — bare infinitive.

Only Gerund (V-ing)Only Infinitive (to + V)Both — different meaning
enjoy, avoid, mind, suggest, deny, admit, consider, imagine, keep, practise, finish, miss, risk, involve, postpone, delay, dislike, can't stand, feel likewant, decide, hope, plan, promise, agree, refuse, offer, expect, learn, manage, afford, seem, pretend, appear, tend, choose, arrangeremember, forget, stop, try, regret, mean, go on, need, like/love/hate (both possible)

The classic traps:
• remember to lock (не забыл сделать) vs remember locking (помню, что сделал)
• stop to smoke (остановился, чтобы покурить) vs stop smoking (бросил курить)
• try to open (постарался) vs try opening (попробовал как эксперимент)
• regret to say (сожалею, что сообщаю) vs regret saying (сожалею, что сказал)

After prepositions — always gerund: good at swimming, interested in learning, afraid of flying, look forward to seeing, get used to living, be used to working.

Block 6 · Numerals

Cardinals · ordinals · dates · years · fractions — spelling drills

Reference · the rules that trip Daniella up

Cardinal dashes: 21 = twenty-one, 42 = forty-two, 99 = ninety-nine. Дефис между десятками и единицами.
Hundred / thousand / million — no -s after a number: two hundred, five thousand. С -s только: hundreds of people.
And — в британском формате перед десятками: 250 = two hundred and fifty.
Ordinal irregulars: first · second · third · fifth · ninth · twelfth · twentieth · twenty-first · thirtieth… (не fiveth / twelveth / twentyth).
Dates (British ЕГЭ): on the 5th of May, 2026. Years: 1876 → eighteen seventy-six; 2005 → two thousand and five; 2026 → twenty twenty-six.
Fractions: ½ = one half / a half; ¾ = three quarters; ⅓ = one third / a third.
Percents: 50% = fifty per cent.

Basic: short (1 syllable) → -er/-est (big / bigger / the biggest). Long (2+ syllables) → more/most (interesting / more interesting / the most interesting). Y → -ier/-iest (happy / happier).

Special structures: as … as (equal) · not as … as / not so … as (less) · the more … the better (parallel) · doubles: bigger and bigger / more and more difficult.

R.7 · Reading · Mendeleev · full understanding · 7 items · EGE 12-18 format

Dmitri Mendeleev was born in 1834 in the small Siberian town of Tobolsk, the youngest of seventeen children. When he was thirteen, his father died and his mother took him thousands of kilometres by horse to Moscow, and then to Saint Petersburg, so that he could receive a proper education. Later Mendeleev would say that everything he became, he owed to that single stubborn journey.

At university he specialised in chemistry, but he was equally interested in agriculture, oil refining and even balloon flight. In 1869, at the age of thirty-five, he did the work that made him world-famous: he arranged all sixty-three known chemical elements into a single table, ordered by atomic weight. What impressed the scientific world most was not the table itself — several other chemists had tried something similar — but the fact that Mendeleev left empty squares, predicting elements that had not yet been discovered. Within twenty years, gallium, scandium and germanium had appeared, exactly where he said they would.

Mendeleev's public life was not always easy. He argued with government ministers, he was refused a professorship in Saint Petersburg for being «too democratic», and his second marriage created a scandal because the Church had not yet approved the divorce from his first wife. But he stayed a working scientist to the end. His last big project was to modernise the Russian system of weights and measures — a boring administrative job that ordinary chemists would have avoided.

Today, more than one hundred and fifty years after his table, it hangs in every chemistry classroom in the world. In Russia itself, Mendeleev is remembered not only for chemistry but for his belief that a scientist has a duty to improve everyday life.
