02

Say it back in full · 12 provocations · production only

📚 Homework

▶ hear a short prompt from Emma or Brian → write a full sentence in the required tense. No multiple choice. Use articles, auxiliaries, one vocab word.

12 prompts · full sentence writing · self-check
"How often does your team meet?" Present Simple
Full sentence. Use: every Monday · team · meeting. Watch the article a / the.
👁 Sample answer

Every Monday my team meets at 10 in the main office.

"What are you doing at your desk right now?" Present Continuous
Full sentence. Use: right now · I am + V-ing. Don't drop am.
👁 Sample answer

Right now I am checking the schedule for tomorrow's negotiation.

"What have you already done today?" Present Perfect · «already» order
Full sentence. Put already BETWEEN have and V3. Use article the.
👁 Sample answer

I have already sent the invoice and answered three emails.

"How long have you been working on the Milan negotiation?" Present Perfect Continuous
Full sentence. Use have been + V-ing + for/since.
👁 Sample answer

I have been working on the Milan negotiation for three months.

"When will you introduce us to the CFO?" Future · will
Full sentence. Use will + V1. Watch the article before CFO.
👁 Sample answer

I will introduce you to the CFO next Monday at 3 p.m.

"What is your plan for improving our sales this quarter?" going to
Full sentence. Use are going to + V1. Plural sales.
👁 Sample answer

We are going to improve our sales by launching two new products this quarter.

"Tell me one thing your company does." Present Simple · 3rd -s
Full sentence. Company = it → covers / makes / sells / offers. Use -s.
👁 Sample answer

My company covers enterprise logistics for the whole region.

"Has the boss approved anything today?" Present Perfect
Full sentence. Use The boss has + V3 + the ___.
👁 Sample answer

The boss has approved our new schedule this morning.

"What is happening in your team at the moment?" Present Continuous
Full sentence. Use At the moment we are + V-ing. Numbers get plural: three operators.
👁 Sample answer

At the moment we are hiring three operators for the night shift.

"How long have you been waiting for the client?" Present Perfect Continuous
Full sentence. Use I have been waiting for + object + since / for.
👁 Sample answer

I have been waiting for the client since 9 in the morning.

"How likely is a Friday signature on the deal?" Future · will · «likely»
Full sentence. Use It is likely (that) we will + V1.
👁 Sample answer

It is likely we will sign the deal on Friday afternoon.

"What are your plans for tomorrow?" going to
Full sentence. Use Tomorrow I am going to + V1. Watch the article before Milan office.
👁 Sample answer

Tomorrow I am going to meet the Milan office team at 11.

04

Negative rewrite · 8 broken sentences → clean English

▶ listen to the correct version, then rewrite. Focus: "I have not new tasks" → "I don't have any new tasks".

8 sentences · your own rewrite
Wrong version: "I have not new tasks today."
I don't have any new tasks today.
Rule: for a "no + noun" idea use don't have any — never have not.
Wrong version: "I don't finish the report."
I haven't finished the report yet.
Present Perfect negative · haven't + V3 + yet. yet at the end.
Wrong version: "We don't sign the deal this week."
We aren't going to sign the deal this week.
Plan cancelled → aren't going to + V1.
Wrong version: "I don't will late for the meeting."
I won't be late for the meeting.
Future negative · won't + V1. Never don't will.
Wrong version: "He don't work on this project right now."
He isn't working on this project right now.
Present Continuous · isn't + V-ing. right now is the marker.
Wrong version: "We not discuss the negotiation for weeks."
We haven't been discussing the negotiation for weeks.
Present Perfect Continuous negative · haven't been + V-ing.
Wrong version: "She not work in finance more."
She doesn't work in the finance team anymore.
Present Simple negative · doesn't + V1 · "not anymore" → put anymore at the end.
Wrong version: "They not approve new schedule."
They haven't approved the new schedule.
Present Perfect · haven't + V3 + article the before a specific schedule.

Milan office scenario · 60 seconds

Brian: Emma, quick check. Have we already sent the logistics plan to Milan?

Emma: Yes, I've already sent it. The Milan office confirmed at 9. They're preparing the negotiation right now.
