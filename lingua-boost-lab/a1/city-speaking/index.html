01

The core formula · everything starts here

Before you start, say each frame out loud. This is your compass for the next 90 minutes.

1 · What exists
There is / There are
There is a park. There are many shops.
2 · The situation
It is
It is cold. It is a big city.
3 · Usually, always
I + V (Present Simple)
I work in the city. I drink coffee every morning.
4 · Right now
I am + V-ing (Continuous)
I am walking now. I am drinking coffee.

One chain of 4 sentences for any place:   «There is a market. It is cold. I wear a coat. I am wearing boots now.»   — build such a chain about your area in 30 seconds. Out loud.

Speaking finale · Round 1 · today

Six unusual questions. Not «describe your city» — specific situations. Answer in 2-3 sentences each. ▶ to hear · ✓ Done when said.

1.A friend from another country is visiting you for one day. Where do you take them? (In the morning I take them to … For lunch we go to … In the evening …)
2.Imagine your city had no cars for one week. What changes? (It is quieter. People are walking. The streets are …)
3.What is one thing in your city that always makes you smile? (There is a … I like it because …)
4.Tell me about a place in your city you do not like. Why? (I do not like … because it is … and there are too many …)
5.What does your street look like at 7 a.m. compared to 7 p.m.? (At 7 a.m. there are … At 7 p.m. it is … and people are …)
6.If a tourist had only one photo to take in your city — what should they photograph? Why? (They should take a photo of … because it is …)
09

Future · will vs going to · rule in 60 seconds

Two ways to talk about future. The difference is not in meaning — it is where the idea came from. Look at the 4 panels above, then read the four frames.

will · decision right now
I will + V
"It is cold." — "OK, I will close the window." (idea was born this second)
will · prediction
It will + V
Robots will work in cafés in 50 years.
going to · existing plan
I am going to + V
I am going to visit my parents on Sunday. (decided earlier)
going to · evidence in front of you
It is going to + V
Look at the sky — it is going to rain. (you see the signs now)

Short rule in one line:

— will = idea born now (decision, promise) OR prediction about the world.
— going to = plan made earlier, OR signs are showing it now.

Native speakers mix these all the time. Start using both — accuracy will follow.

Will-drill · 6 decisions and predictions

Read the situation out loud and react with will. No preparation — first thing that comes to mind. ▶ to hear the prompt. ✓ Done when said.

1.The doorbell is ringing. What will you do? (I will open … / I will check who it is …)
2.I say: "I am thirsty." What will you offer me? (I will bring you water … / I will make tea …)
3.What will the weather be like tomorrow? (It will be … I think it will …)
4.What will cities look like in 100 years? Two predictions. (Cities will be … People will …)
5.Promise me one thing for next week. (I will … I promise.)
6.Will people still read paper books in 50 years? Why or why not? (Yes, they will … / No, they will not because …)

Grammar drill · will or won't

will / won't. We use will for predictions and decisions made at the moment of speaking. I'll be at home tonight. / I won't drink coffee — I won't sleep.
'll = will · won't = will not.

Click will or won't for each sentence. Wrong click = red. Right = green.

1. Don't drink coffee before bed. You ___ sleep.
→ Prediction (negative consequence) → won't
2. "Are you ready?" — "Not yet. I ___ be ready in five minutes."
→ Decision / promise about the future → will
3. I'm away for a few days, so I ___ be at home tomorrow.
→ Negative prediction → won't
4. It ___ rain — you don't need an umbrella.
→ Confident negative prediction → won't
5. Go to bed early and you ___ feel better in the morning.
→ Positive prediction → will
6. It's Bill's birthday on Monday. He ___ be 25.
→ Future fact (he turns 25) → will
7. I'm sorry I was late. It ___ happen again.
→ Promise (won't repeat) → won't

Going-to-drill · 6 plans you've already made

Tell me what you already decided earlier. Use going to. Full sentences. ▶ to hear the question.

1.What are you going to do tonight? (I am going to … then I am going to …)
2.What are you going to do this weekend? Two specific plans. (On Saturday I am going to … On Sunday …)
3.Are you going to travel anywhere this summer? (Yes, I am going to visit … / No, I am going to stay in …)
4.Look outside. Is it going to rain or be sunny in the next hour? (It is going to … because I can see …)
5.What are you going to learn next month in English? (I am going to learn … and …)
6.What is the biggest thing you are going to do this year? (I am going to … because …)

Grammar drill · will or going to · which is right?

Going to = the plan or arrangement existed before the moment of speaking. "I am going to visit my parents on Sunday." (decided earlier)
Will = the decision is made now, or it's a prediction. "I will close the window — it's cold."
Also: present continuous for definite arrangements: "We're going to the cinema on Saturday — we have tickets."

Choose the right form. Wrong click → red + the explanation appears.

1. We ___ the theatre tonight. We've got tickets.
→ Tickets already bought = plan made earlier → are going to
2. "What ___ tomorrow evening?" — "Nothing. I'm free."
→ Asking about specific arrangements → are you doing
3. They ___ away tomorrow morning. Their train is at 8.40.
→ Train booked, definite plan → are going
4. I'm sure she ___ us some money. She's very rich.
→ Prediction (your opinion), no fixed arrangement → will lend
5. "Why are you putting on your coat?" — "I ___ out."
→ You decided already (coat is on) → am going
6. Do you think Claire ___ us tonight?
→ Prediction / wondering → will phone
7. Steve can't meet us on Saturday. He ___.
→ Already in his schedule → is working
8. ___ you be at home tomorrow evening?
→ Question about future state → Will
