Today's only grammar focus · asking politely

When something is wrong, you need polite help. Three magic openings. Every scene below uses them.

Can I have…? (asking to receive) Could you…? (asking the other person) I need… (saying what you need)
"Can I have something for a headache, please?"
"Could you help me, please? I don't feel well."
"I need an ambulance. My friend is sick."
Health note · Canada
For a mild symptom — a headache, a cough, a runny nose — most Canadians go to a pharmacy first. Pharmacists give advice and simple medicine without a prescription. For a fever over 39 °C or pain that will not stop, go to a walk-in clinic. For chest pain, breathing trouble, or a bad fall — call 911.

Health note · headaches
Adults get 2 to 3 headaches a month on average. Most are tension headaches — from stress, long screen time, or not enough water. First aid at home: a big glass of water, fresh air, and 15 minutes of rest. See a doctor the same day if a headache is very strong on one side, comes with a fever, or you cannot see well.

Bonus · the next morning · Bella has a rash

A small extra scenario — an allergic reaction. New words: rash, itchy, allergic reaction, antihistamines.

Pharmacist: Hi again. How are you feeling today?

Bella: Better, thank you. But now I have a red rash on my arms. It's very itchy.

Scene 3 · calling 911Emergency

On her way home, Bella sees an older man fall on the sidewalk. She calls 911 — the Canadian emergency number.
🚨
Bella is on Queen Street. An older man is on the ground, holding his chest. People stop, but no one acts. Bella takes her phone and calls 911.
Step 1 · Vocabulary
Step 2 · Dialogue · read out loud (Bella → you)

Operator: 911, what is your emergency?

Bella: I need an ambulance, please. There is a man on the street. He fell down.

Operator: Where are you, ma'am?

Bella: I'm on Queen Street, near the post office. The man is holding his chest.

Operator: Is he conscious? Is he breathing?

Bella: Yes, he is breathing. But he looks very weak.

Operator: Help is on the way. Could you stay with him until they arrive?

Bella: Yes, I'll stay. Please hurry.

Step 3 · Controlled drill · choose the right word
Step 4 · Your turn · use the template

You see an emergency. Call 911. Use four short sentences.

I need ____ (an ambulance / the police / the fire department), please. There is ____ (a man / a woman / a child) ____ (on the street / in the building / by the lake). I'm on ____ (___ Street, near ___). ____ (He / She) is breathing but ____ (very weak / unconscious / hurt). Please hurry.
Practice slow + clear. In emergency English, calm voice = fast help.

📞 Dialogue · Bella books and joins a virtual visit

Receptionist: Maple Health Clinic, how can I help?

Bella: Hi. I'd like to book a telehealth appointment, please. I have a sore throat and a runny nose.

Receptionist: Sure. Our next slot is in 30 minutes. We'll send you a link by text — just click it five minutes before.

Bella: Perfect. Do I need to upload anything before the visit?

Receptionist: If you have a photo of your throat, upload it through the patient app. Otherwise the doctor will ask you to show it on camera.

— 30 minutes later, video call —

Doctor: Hi Bella, can you hear me OK?

Bella: Yes, loud and clear. I think my microphone was muted at first — sorry about that.

Doctor: No problem. Can you open your mouth and tilt the camera to show me your throat?

Bella: Like this?

Doctor: Perfect. Yes, your throat looks red but not infected. I'll send an electronic prescription to your pharmacy. Take it for five days.

Bella: Thank you! Should I book a follow-up?

Doctor: Only if it gets worse or doesn't improve in a week. Feel better soon.

✅ Pros and cons · think and discuss

👍 Pros
  • No travel — save time and energy when ill
  • Quick access to a doctor (often same day)
  • Electronic prescription goes straight to your pharmacy
  • Easier for people with limited mobility
  • Lower cost than an in-person visit
👎 Cons
  • Doctor can't physically examine you (no palpation, no listening to lungs)
  • Tech problems — screen freezes, audio drops, login fails
  • Not suitable for emergencies or serious conditions
  • Some elderly patients struggle with apps and devices
  • Privacy concerns — health data online
