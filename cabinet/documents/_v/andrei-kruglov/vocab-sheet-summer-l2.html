1 · «As It Seems» — C1 hedges 5 items

The single feature that lifts a band 4.5 essay/speech to band 5.0. Weave one hedge into every C1 answer, never two in the same sentence — this is register, not decoration.

HedgeRegisterExample
seeminglyadverbThe policy has produced seemingly contradictory outcomes across regions.Signals “on the surface — but let me qualify”.
apparentlyadverbApparently, the correlation weakens once income is controlled for.Attributes the claim to visible evidence, not personal conviction.
it appears that…sentence starterIt appears that reef sections recovering fastest also had the lowest local pollution.Formal opener; safer than “I think that”.
one might argue (that)…discussion openerOne might argue that summer tourism has already reached a structural limit.Introduces a stance without committing to it.
to a certain extentquantifier hedgeAutomation offsets labour shortages to a certain extent, but not in specialised trades.Partial agreement — powerful concession move.

Practical, transferable, TOEFL-friendly. Ten adjectives that instantly move Andrew from B2 to C1 the moment a native asks «how's the weather over there?»

AdjectiveWhat it namesExample

breezyWith a light, pleasant wind.«A breezy afternoon at the coast.»

3 · C1 academic core · flip-card set 16 items

Not «summer trivia» — these are the register-lifters that show up in every TOEFL reading, listening and speaking task. Learn one row, use it in one week.

TermPOSMeaning · example
pivotaladjAbsolutely central, decisive (upgrade of «very important»).Peer review plays a pivotal role in academic publishing.
to underscoreverbTo emphasise formally (upgrade of «to show» / «to stress»).The 2023 data underscore the urgency of adaptation policy.
to give rise tophraseTo cause / produce (formal, essay-friendly).Overreliance on air travel gives rise to significant emissions.
notwithstandingconnectorDespite / in spite of (high-C1 register).Notwithstanding the cost, the policy has broad public support.
to a certain extenthedgePartly, not fully — softens a claim.To a certain extent, technology can offset labour shortages.
arguablyhedgeIt can be argued that — sounds informed, not dogmatic.Arguably, the most valuable asset of any firm is its people.
compelling evidencecollocationVery strong evidence that is hard to reject.There is compelling evidence that early intervention improves outcomes.
to strike a balance betweencollocationTo find the right proportion of two competing things.It is crucial to strike a balance between growth and sustainability.
overriding concerncollocationThe most important worry, more important than all others.Data privacy remains the overriding concern in this partnership.
far-reaching implicationscollocationConsequences that go well beyond the immediate case.The court's ruling has far-reaching implications for future contracts.
to constituteverbTo form / to be (upgrade of plain «to be»).Renewable sources now constitute a third of national output.
to warrantverbTo justify / to make necessary (formal).These findings warrant a full independent review.
discernibleadjNoticeable, able to be perceived (formal upgrade of «visible»).There is a discernible shift in consumer preferences.
to leverageverb (biz)To use something to maximum advantage.The company leveraged its brand to enter new markets.
stakeholdernoun (biz)Any party with an interest in the outcome — investors, staff, customers, community.Every major decision requires stakeholder consultation.
trade-offnoun (biz/acad)A compromise where you accept a loss on one side to gain on another.There is always a trade-off between speed and accuracy.

Summer Systems · Reading 1

Reading 1 · Heat mortality

Heat mortality, urban heat island, public-health register — the language Andrew needs to write and speak about climate impact at band 5.0+.

The passage · highlighted collocations

In recent decades, epidemiologists have documented a striking pattern across Southern Europe: heat has quietly overtaken cold as the leading weather-related cause of excess mortality. A large 2023 Lancet study estimated that more than 61 000 people died from the 2022 European summer heatwaves — a figure that would have seemed almost unthinkable a generation ago.

However, the impact is uneven. Elderly residents in dense inner-city neighbourhoods, where the urban heat island effect intensifies night-time temperatures, are far more vulnerable than younger populations in rural areas. Chronic cardiovascular and respiratory conditions are often the underlying cause; heat is the trigger.

Public-health experts increasingly argue that summer heat should be treated as a permanent infrastructure problem, not an occasional emergency — requiring cool centres, shaded transit and building retrofits, rather than short-term warnings alone.

4 · Key TOEFL / public-health terms 7 items

TermWhat it means · why it matters
excess mortalityDeaths beyond the statistically expected level.Standard metric in every epidemiology paper.
urban heat island effectCities becoming several degrees hotter than surrounding countryside — asphalt + buildings + traffic.TOEFL classic; also IELTS Task 1.
underlying cause · triggerMedicine + disaster analysis pair: chronic reason vs. immediate event.Strong in cause-and-effect writing.
chronic cardiovascular / respiratory conditionsLong-term diseases of the heart / lungs.Any health-policy passage uses this pair.
infrastructure problemStructural, permanent issue needing physical investment.Reframe device: turns an “event” into a “system”.
building retrofitsModifying existing buildings for a new purpose (here: heat resilience).Every sustainability essay.
shaded transit / cool centresAdaptive urban solutions — cooled public spaces, shaded transport routes.Concrete examples for essay body.

5 · C1 academic collocations · reading 1 10 items

CollocationRegister · usage
to document a patternFormal / academic verb for “to record and describe systematically”.Upgrades any plain “to show”.
a striking patternAdj + noun. Neutral academic.Alt.: a marked / a noticeable pattern.
to overtake (as leading cause)Statistical language: to become bigger than a previous thing.Bonus adverb: “quietly overtake”.
the leading … cause of XRanking template — “the leading dietary cause of heart disease”.Universal.
the impact is unevenHedge in equality / policy writing.Perfect topic-sentence follow-up.
far more vulnerable thanComparative + risk vocab.Also: markedly / significantly more vulnerable than.
to intensifyC1 verb — upgrade of “to make stronger”.Objects: heat, competition, scrutiny, debate.
to be treated as X, not YReframing device — argues for a change of category.“treated as a right, not a privilege”.
rather than … aloneConcession + insistence structure.“Regulation, rather than voluntary codes alone, is required.”
experts increasingly argue that…Academic sentence-starter with soft hedge.Alt.: “There is growing agreement that…”.

6 · Sentence starters & hedges 5 items

  • In recent decades, … — sets an academic timeframe; safer than “nowadays”.
  • A large 2023 study estimated that … — cite research in speaking / writing without a full reference.
  • A figure that would have seemed almost unthinkable a generation ago … — dramatic hedge that still sounds sober.
  • However, the impact is uneven. — one-sentence pivot from general claim to nuance.
  • Public-health experts increasingly argue that … — attribute the claim without owning it.
Mini-homework · 3 tasks · 20 min
  1. Rewrite this sentence in a more C1 register: «More people are dying from summer heat now.» Use at least 3 items from Section 4 or 5.
  2. Write one paragraph (4–5 sentences) on «Why cities are harder to cool than countryside.» — required: urban heat island effect + far more vulnerable than + one hedge from Section 1.
  3. Speaking: record yourself for 45 seconds answering «Should summer heat be treated as an infrastructure problem?» — start with Public-health experts increasingly argue that… and close with rather than … alone.
